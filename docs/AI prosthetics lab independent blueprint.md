# Repair retrieval, verify sentences, then expand domains

The most efficient, evidence-supported way to build AI Prosthetic Lab is to keep the prototype's fixed retrieval-plus-writer pipeline (its word-for-word quote check, its single swap point for the writing model and its 59-statement answer key) and grow it in place, in a strict order: repair retrieval, add verification and abstention decided by code, make a local writer a first-class path, and only then add orthotics, gait and rehabilitation as configuration-driven domain packs. Retrieval is the binding constraint: only **129 of 359 (36%)** expected answer-key statements appear in the six passages per topic that the writer sees, and even thirty passages reach only 51%, so a free offline miss classifier should decide between re-parsing documents (Docling first, starting with the never-retrieved WHO manual), re-chunking by document structure with heading paths, and adding a BM25 leg fused with bge-m3, before any model is swapped. The dominant documented failure of cited answers is not an invented quote but a real quote attached to a sentence it does not support (**50–90% of medical LLM responses not fully supported** by their cited sources), and models rarely abstain on their own (**9 of 12 scored 0%** on unanswerable medical questions), so a deterministic fidelity check, a small local entailment checker and a code-decided "No sufficient evidence identified" belong ahead of any new domain. Anthropic, OpenAI and Google do not list Syria among supported countries, so the owner's swap-point design should be used now: a local writer (Qwen3-8B, Qwen3.5-9B or Gemma 4 E4B under llama.cpp's Windows CUDA 13 server, thinking off, schema-constrained) with bge-m3 moved to the CPU and no silent cloud fallback. Clinically, the system will often have to report that evidence is weak: the anchor VA/DoD 2025 amputation guideline has **26 recommendations and none strong**, no graded guideline positively recommends any socket design, no source tests readiness-for-fitting criteria, and orthotics rests on five graded guideline areas plus mostly low-certainty reviews. Licences, not relevance, decide what can be quoted: US federal guidance, CC BY reviews and WHO's bilingual P&O Standards can be ingested in full, while ISO forbids any AI use of its text and Cochrane reserves text-mining and AI-training rights. Arabic should be a checked rendering of verified English, driven by a reviewed glossary and deterministic checks on numbers, side, level, negation and modality, because no Arabic clinical grounding checker and no shippable Arabic P&O glossary were found. Nothing here is validated yet: with 13 golden cases, even zero critical errors only bounds the true critical-error rate below about 21%.

**How to read this report.** Every claim carries one of four labels, which applies to the sentence it opens and to the following sentences until the next label: **[DIRECT]** means stated in the cited source; **[INFERRED]** means reasoning from cited facts; **[PROPOSED]** means a design recommendation for the owner to accept or reject, not a finding; **[UNKNOWN]** means searched for and not established. Where nothing was found, the report says "No sufficient evidence identified". All proposals respect the owner's decisions recorded in the 11 October 2026 handoff: retrieval plus a model writing sentences backed by word-for-word checked quotes is the product direction; the 59 hand-written statements are the answer key the RAG is measured against; the model reports what sources say and never advises; the specialist decides; and clinical evidence values come only from the owner ([handoff, 11 Oct 2026](/root/.claude/uploads/830110ea-030d-5d0c-9ab6-615062c1589f/85794c40-handoff_prompt_2026-10-11.md)). ## 1. Keep the quote-checked pipeline and grow it in place

[DIRECT] On 11 October 2026 the owner turned the prototype from a rule engine into a RAG system: search stays local with the bge-m3 embedding model, one cloud model (Claude, `claude-opus-5-5`, with server-side fallbacks) writes short English and Arabic sentences each carrying an exact quote and a passage id, code checks every quote word for word with a five-word minimum, and the 59 hand-written statements stopped producing the review page and became the answer key ([handoff](/root/.claude/uploads/830110ea-030d-5d0c-9ab6-615062c1589f/85794c40-handoff_prompt_2026-10-11.md)). Only the prosthetics domain exists: use case 01 (which intake information is missing and why the sources say it matters), use case 02 (functional level and what sources say per component) and design and fitting support, all over fake cases, with an append-only review log, **368 passing tests** and **13 golden cases, 3 of them reviewed**. The live answers, misquote rate, time per case and cost were never measured, because no API key was on the machine, and the Arabic interface text was written by Claude and has not been checked by an Arabic-reading prosthetist.

[DIRECT] The 2024–2026 evidence supports keeping that direction. A JAMIA meta-analysis of 20 studies found a pooled odds ratio of **1.35 (95% CI 1.19–1.53)** for RAG against baseline LLMs ([Liu et al., JAMIA 2025](https://doi.org/10.1093/jamia/ocaf008)); a general-domain preprint found RAG consistently beat unsupervised fine-tuning for injecting knowledge ([Ovadia et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.05934)); and on GraphRAG-Bench's medical corpus, plain RAG with reranking beat Microsoft GraphRAG on fact retrieval (**64.73 vs 38.63**) while using about **954 tokens per query against 332,881** for global GraphRAG ([GraphRAG-Bench, ICLR 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.05690v3)). Plain RAG also handled questions that should get no answer far better than graph variants (96.01 vs 80.07 at 8B) ([Han et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.11371v2)). Vendor guidance recommends "the simplest solution" and fixed workflows over agents ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/building-effective-agents)), the best model on MedAgentBench completed only 69.67% of tasks ([MedAgentBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.14654)), and gains from multi-agent systems "are often minimal" ([MAST](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.13657)). [INFERRED] The prototype is therefore already the right shape: a neuro-symbolic hybrid in which deterministic parts (the answer key, the forbidden-output rules, applicability gates such as "K-level only for lower limb") surround a neural retriever and writer. Its binding constraint is retrieval recall, not reasoning depth. [UNKNOWN] No controlled comparison of RAG, rule engines and neuro-symbolic hybrids in prosthetics, orthotics, gait or rehabilitation was found.

[PROPOSED] The recommended build, component by component, is below; each row is developed in the section named in the last column.

[INFERRED] The order matters. Retrieval comes first because every downstream metric is bounded by it: a writer cannot quote a passage it never receives, so comparing writers before fixing retrieval mostly measures retrieval. Verification and abstention come next because they address the three largest documented risks (unsupported summaries behind real quotes, models that do not abstain, and clinicians who follow fluent wrong output). The local writer moves from a late phase (where the architecture research track placed it) to the present, because Anthropic, OpenAI and Google do not list Syria as a supported country ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/supported-countries); [OpenAI](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/supported-countries); [Google](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/available-regions)), which makes local-first a requirement rather than a preference if the developer works from Syria. New domains come last: each needs its own answer key, golden cases and specialist reviewers, and adding them before the shared engine is verified multiplies unverified output. Section 16 turns this order into gated phases with acceptance tests.


## 2. Retrieval caps everything: only 36% of answer-key statements reach the writer

[DIRECT] The owner's free measurement with the real local search asked whether each golden case's expected answer-key statements sit inside any passage the topic search returns. At the current six passages per topic the answer is **129 of 359 (36%)**, rising to 40% at 10, 41% at 15, 47% at 20 and **51% at 30**; the weakest topics are alignment (10/66), socket (2/23), suspension (4/26) and fitting (14/54); and the four WHO statements are never found, because the WHO manual is searched but never ranks in the top six ([handoff](/root/.claude/uploads/830110ea-030d-5d0c-9ab6-615062c1589f/85794c40-handoff_prompt_2026-10-11.md)). [UNKNOWN] The exact matching rule behind the 36% (scratch scripts were used) is not documented, so the baseline should be recomputed with one shared normaliser before anything is changed.

[INFERRED] The depth curve points to an index or representation problem rather than a choice of k: going from six to thirty passages adds only 15 points, so about half of the (case, statement) pairs either rank below 30 or cannot be retrieved as a contained quote at all. The WHO pattern points away from simple crowding, since a long manual has many chunks and still never surfaces. The notes offer four candidate causes, and they need to be separated by experiment rather than argued: extraction or layout noise; a low learned "prior" for that writing style, since supervised dense retrievers systematically under-retrieve some genuinely relevant documents and niche or technical content tends to be disfavoured ([Valentini et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.02814)); position and brevity biases, under which the answer-containing document wins "less than 10%" of the time when biases combine ([Fayyaz et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.05037v2)); and query wording, because every topic query names the US-coding K-level and the side, which may pull rankings toward CMS coverage documents (a plausible, untested hypothesis). A fifth, weaker explanation is that the WHO Standards describe service systems rather than components, so they rarely match component queries ([WHO P&O Standards](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789241512480)).

[PROPOSED] The first fix is therefore a diagnostic: a free, offline script that runs once per (case, topic, expected statement) pair over the existing index, takes about half a day to a day to write, assigns the first matching class below while recording every flag, and adds a per-document "retrievability" table (the share of all topic queries in which each document reaches the top 6 and the top 30). The largest miss class decides the next step. [UNKNOWN] The share of each class among the 230 missed pairs is unknown until the script runs, and every downstream priority depends on it.

| # | Miss class | Test | Usual fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Normalisation artefact | `find_quote`'s normaliser finds the quote but the measurement script does not | One shared normaliser everywhere |
| 2 | Extraction lost | Normalised quote absent from the extracted text (retry with de-hyphenation, ligature folding, header/footer stripping) | Re-parse that document |
| 3 | Split across chunks | Quote present in the text but not inside any single chunk | Structure-aware chunks, overlap, token-coverage scoring |
| 4 | Ranked low (near 7–30 / far >30) | Rank of the best gold-containing chunk in a full ranking | Near: hybrid, reranker, k. Far: classes 6–7 |
| 5 | Crowded out | Miss while ≥4 of the top 6 come from one other document | Per-document cap or MMR |
| 6 | Query mismatch | An oracle query (the gold quote itself, or the answer key's own sentence) ranks the gold chunk ≤6 but the topic query does not | Query templates, held-out cases only |
| 7 | Representation problem | Even the literal-quote oracle ranks the gold chunk >6 | Smaller structure-aligned chunks, cleaner extraction, heading-path prefix |
| 8 | Equivalent evidence elsewhere | Top-k holds another document stating the same fact | Adjudicate by hand; add as dated alternative gold |

[DIRECT] The taxonomy is a synthesis of published failure points ([Barnett et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.05856v1)), span-level evaluation against exact-match excerpts, which is the same shape as the owner's answer key ([Chroma](https://www.trychroma.com/research/evaluating-chunking)), and the redundancy problem that can undervalue a retriever that finds equivalent evidence ([RARE, ACL 2026](https://preview.aclanthology.org/ingest-acl/2026.acl-long.923/)); no validated span-level miss procedure was found. [PROPOSED] Every run should report pair coverage (x/359) and unique-statement coverage (y/59), token coverage at k, retrieved tokens per topic, gold rank and MRR@30, coverage at a fixed token budget (such as 1,500 retrieved tokens per topic), per-topic and per-document tables, the share of retrieved chunks that are gold for anything (Judged@6), and a flip list of statements gained and lost. [DIRECT] Paired t-tests are "remarkably robust" and recommended for comparing systems, while bootstrap-shift and Wilcoxon tests "should be discontinued" ([Urbano et al., SIGIR 2019](https://ar5iv.org/html/1905.11096)); ranx (MIT) implements them and RRF ([ranx](https://github.com/AmenRa/ranx)). [INFERRED] The 359 pairs are 59 statements repeated across 13 cases, not 359 independent observations, so small differences are noise and both counts must be reported. [DIRECT] Configurations that looked strong on synthetic queries "drop substantially" on authentic ones, and optimising on synthetic queries picked a hybrid with up to 8× the latency ([Kucia & Gawlik 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.14579)). [PROPOSED] Ingestion changes (parser, chunker, heading prefix) are corpus-wide and can be measured on all 13 cases, but query-template changes are evaluated only on frozen or newly written cases (5–10 new fake cases with answer-key entries, ideally written by someone other than the tuner), later joined by authentic queries from the planned prosthetist trial. The answer key is corrected only for verified errors, dated, because the 10 unreviewed golden cases may themselves contain errors that lower measured recall.

### Parse only the problem documents, and keep the PDF's own characters

[DIRECT] No parsing benchmark covers clinical guidelines or manuals or measures retrieval recall. OmniDocBench gives a third of its overall score to formula recognition ([OmniDocBench](https://github.com/opendatalab/OmniDocBench)), and an independent CPU-only run of olmOCR-Bench on a four-core runner scored MinerU's Basic tier **74.6 (6.2 s/page, 4.6 GB RAM)**, Docling **53.4 (4.3 s/page)**, marker in fast mode 58.3 (24.2 s/page) and pymupdf4llm 37.5 (0.85 s/page) ([pdf-cpu-bench](https://github.com/intikhab49/pdf-cpu-bench)). Those numbers conflict with Marker's self-reported table, which gives Docling 50.3 and liteparse 22.4 against the independent 53.4 and 40.6 ([Marker README](https://github.com/datalab-to/marker)), and the OHRBench authors conclude that none of the OCR solutions they tested "is competent for constructing high-quality knowledge bases for RAG systems" ([OHRBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.02592)). [INFERRED] Vision-language parsers top the leaderboards but re-generate text from page images, which can introduce "semantic noise" that silently breaks the word-for-word quote check and page location, so born-digital PDFs should be parsed from their text layer and OCR kept for pages without one. On an 8 GB card under Windows, olmOCR 2 is excluded (it needs at least 12 GB of VRAM and installs on Ubuntu or Debian only) ([olmOCR](https://github.com/allenai/olmocr)), MinerU's PyTorch tiers need 8 GB or more, which is borderline, and Marker's GPU path needs Docker with the NVIDIA Container Toolkit.

[DIRECT] Docling has the cleanest licence for a project that may later become a service: MIT code ([Docling](https://github.com/docling-project/docling)), Apache-2.0 layout weights with page-header, page-footer, footnote, section-header and table classes ([Heron layout model](https://huggingface.co/docling-project/docling-layout-heron)), Windows support and Python 3.14 classifiers ([PyPI docling](https://pypi.org/project/docling/)). MinerU's licence adds conditions to Apache-2.0: online services must "clearly and prominently" show that MinerU is used, a separate licence is needed above 100 million monthly users or USD 20 million monthly revenue, and rights "terminate automatically" if either condition is breached ([MinerU licence](https://github.com/opendatalab/MinerU/blob/master/LICENSE.md)); pymupdf4llm's layout package is AGPL-3.0 unless a commercial licence is bought ([PyPI pymupdf-layout](https://pypi.org/project/pymupdf-layout/)). [UNKNOWN] Whether Docling's standard pipeline reads the PDF text layer rather than running OCR was not verified and must be checked on one file; which extractor produced the prototype's current text files, and whether any source PDF is scanned, must be read from the repository. [PROPOSED] Re-parse only the documents that never surface (the WHO manual first, then the sources behind the socket, suspension and alignment misses) with Docling's standard pipeline, keep MinerU Basic as a second opinion, and accept the new text only if each gold quote is still present word for word, running headers and page numbers are gone, reading order around each quote is intact, tables come out as rows with repeated headers, and page numbers are kept so that `locate_pages` still works.

### Chunk by structure and keep two texts per chunk

[DIRECT] Controlled 2024–2026 studies agree that simple structure-based chunking is the most consistent choice for corpus-wide search: across six segmentation methods and four embedders, paragraph segmentation led and proposition chunking was weakest (for example nDCG@10 0.4997 vs 0.3883 with E5-large), "structure-based methods are the clear first choice", and late chunking hurt in-document retrieval by 53% on average for one model ([Zhou et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.16974v1)). The one study that included bge-m3 (36 methods, five embedders, a health domain among six) also favoured grouping paragraphs, though an LLM judged relevance ([Shaukat et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.06976)), and computationally expensive methods "rarely provide consistent gains" ([Caspari et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16586v1)). Real heading paths beat a shuffled-heading placebo by small but significant margins (+0.010 and +0.016 coverage-nDCG@10), while naive two-stage section-then-chunk retrieval did worse; this comes from the abstract of an initial draft, because the full text returned HTTP 429 ([Kuehlkamp et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10170)). A vendor report found LLM-written chunk context cut top-20 retrieval failures from 5.7% to 3.7%, to 2.9% with BM25 added and to 1.9% with reranking ([Anthropic, Contextual Retrieval](https://www.anthropic.com/news/contextual-retrieval)). The only clinical chunking study (four rhinoplasty textbooks, 30 questions) reported adaptive chunks far ahead of fixed 1,000-character windows (F1 0.64 vs 0.24) but carries no inter-rater agreement and possible circularity ([Gomez-Cabello et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12649634)). Docling's HybridChunker merges small chunks under the same heading and repeats table headers when a table spans chunks ([Docling chunking](https://docling-project.github.io/docling/concepts/chunking/)).

[INFERRED] The current "quote is inside a passage" metric favours bigger chunks regardless of ranking quality, so chunking comparisons must also report token coverage and coverage at an equal token budget; otherwise "more recall" can simply mean "more tokens sent to a paid model". [PROPOSED] If quotes split across chunks in more than about a tenth of misses, re-chunk by paragraph or section to roughly 300–500 bge-m3 tokens, sentence-aware, with little or no overlap where chunks align with sections. Keep **two texts per chunk**: the source text, with the PDF's exact characters, used for display and for `find_quote`; and an index text, made of `Document title > Section > Subsection` plus the chunk, used only for embedding and BM25. The quote check must never run against the index text, or the model could "quote" a heading or a generated question as if the source had said it, which would break the owner's principle that every sentence is backed by the source's own words. Metadata such as document type, year, evidence layer and language belong in filter fields, not in the embedded text, because no evidence was found that embedding such tags raises recall.

[INFERRED] A second lever is new content. The legally clean sources verified for the weakest topics contain sentences shaped like answer-key items: the VA/DoD 2025 clinical support tool on gait analysis pairs about 22 deviations with patient and prosthetic causes, the residual-limb tool gives sock-ply and wear-time rules, and the alignment review and the subischial socket trial state findings in short sentences ([VA/DoD Gait Analysis tool](https://www.healthquality.va.gov/HEALTHQUALITY/guidelines/Rehab/amp/LLA-CST-03-04182025-Gait-Analysis-Final-Draft-508.pdf); [Jonkergouw et al. 2016](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0167466); [Loiret et al. 2026](https://www.mdpi.com/2673-1592/8/1/5)). [PROPOSED] Ingest such tables one row per chunk so that short topic queries can match them, then re-baseline; this is an untested hypothesis and must be measured with the answer key, not assumed.

[DIRECT] For Arabic queries, bge-m3's dense mode loses almost nothing across languages: on real Arabic–English corporate benchmarks, Arabic question to English document Hit@20 was 89% against 89% same-language (legal) and 91% against 93% (travel), while the large drops ran the other way, English question to Arabic document (56%), and for multilingual-E5 (51–55%) ([Amiraz et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.07543v2)). Lexical search cannot cross languages: in English–French scientific retrieval, BM25 rose from MAP 0.181 untranslated to 0.390 with query translation and 0.549 with document translation, while the best dense model needed no translation ([CLIRudit](https://aclanthology.org/2025.mrl-main.16.pdf)). [INFERRED] Because topic queries are generated in English from structured fields, the Arabic retrieval problem is confined to free-text input and smart search. [PROPOSED] Measure it directly with Arabic versions of the 13 golden cases' topic queries, translated and checked by an Arabic-reading reviewer, and add query translation only for a lexical leg or if the gap proves material.

[INFERRED] Four tempting moves should wait. Raising k adds paid input tokens for small gains (36% to 40% at k = 10); semantic, LLM-guided and proposition chunking cost more with inconsistent evidence; document-then-passage retrieval hurt in the only controlled test and adds an unrecoverable first-stage failure; and re-OCRing born-digital PDFs with a vision model risks the verbatim quotes the whole product rests on.

[DIRECT] A lexical leg is worth adding because it fails on different queries. BM25 beat bge-m3 dense on R2MED's case-report tasks (PMC-Clinical 23.3 vs 11.4; PMC-Treatment 20.2 vs 17.9) and on SciFact, but scores 4.8 for Arabic questions over English passages ([MTEB results](https://github.com/embeddings-benchmark/results)). On Windows with Python 3.14, bm25s is pure Python with NumPy only, but its default token pattern drops one-character tokens ([bm25s 0.3.13](https://pypi.org/project/bm25s/0.3.13/)), and CPython's Windows build ships SQLite with FTS5 and its `bm25()` ranking ([CPython 3.14 build file](https://github.com/python/cpython/blob/3.14/PCbuild/sqlite3.vcxproj)); rank-bm25 has had no release since 2022 ([PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/rank-bm25/)) and Pyserini ships only as a source distribution ([PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/pyserini/)). Word-level BM25 also beat a subword variant on biomedical text (32.5 vs 21.1 on NanoNFCorpus). [INFERRED] With default tokenisation "K-level" becomes "level" and "pin-lock" becomes "pin" and "lock". [PROPOSED] Use a token pattern that keeps one-character tokens plus a small owner-approved abbreviation map (TSB ↔ total surface bearing, PTB ↔ patellar tendon bearing, SACH ↔ solid ankle cushion heel, transtibial ↔ below-knee, transfemoral ↔ above-knee), send Arabic free text to the dense leg as typed and to BM25 only after translation through the term sheet or a model, and log every translation.

[DIRECT] For fusion, convex combination of normalised scores beat reciprocal rank fusion in- and out-of-domain and can be tuned from few examples, while RRF is parameter-sensitive (a pre-2024 study) ([Bruch et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.11934)); MedRAG found small but consistent gains from fusing BM25 with a dense retriever (QA accuracy over its PubMed corpus 70.26 for the fused pair against 69.23 and 69.38 for each alone) ([MedRAG](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.13178v2)). [PROPOSED] Start with RRF, which needs no tuning, try convex combination with one weight chosen by cross-validation on development statements only, and skip learned fusion with only 59 distinct statements.

[DIRECT] Rerankers can hurt a strong first stage. In Qwen's own table, reranking the top 100 from Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B (61.82 MTEB-R) gave 65.80 with Qwen3-Reranker-0.6B but **57.03 with bge-reranker-v2-m3, below the first stage** ([Qwen3-Reranker card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-Reranker-0.6B)), and on R2MED all three rerankers "fail to improve and can degrade results" over the strongest retriever ([R2MED](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.14558v1)). A vendor report found passing 20 reranked chunks beat 10 or 5 ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/contextual-retrieval)); jina-reranker-v3 is non-commercial. [INFERRED] A reranker can only reorder what it receives, so candidate coverage at depth 50–100 must be measured first, and "no reranker" is a real competitor. bge-m3's `compute_score` over dense, sparse and multi-vector modes can rerank a fused top 50 with no new model and no stored token vectors. [UNKNOWN] Reranker latency and VRAM on an RTX 5060 were not found anywhere and must be measured.

[DIRECT] On the query side, LLM expansion such as HyDE lifted BM25 on reasoning-heavy medical queries from 15.13 to 26.38 nDCG@10 with a 7B generator ([R2MED](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.14558v1)), but across 24 retrievers and 12 datasets "expansion improves scores for weaker models, but generally harms stronger models" ([Weller et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.08541)), and it degrades when the generating model lacks domain knowledge ([Abe et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.12694)). [PROPOSED] Test three deterministic variants per topic, embedded in the same call and fused by RRF: today's template, the template without K-level and side, and that variant plus owner-approved glossary synonyms sent to BM25 only. Try HyDE only on the BM25 leg as a separately costed run, and freeze templates before any scoring on the answer key. Defer fine-tuning the embedder on synthetic queries: published gains of +2 to +9 points are similar to a model swap, synthetic queries made from passages behind answer-key quotes would leak the test into training, and English-only tuning may damage Arabic ability ([GPL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.07577); [CustomIR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.21729)).

[DIRECT] At 10³–10⁵ chunks, exact search is simple and accurate: only flat FAISS indexes "guarantee exact results" ([FAISS](https://github.com/facebookresearch/faiss/wiki/Guidelines-to-choose-an-index)), faiss-cpu has Python 3.14 Windows wheels, and sqlite-vec is still pre-1.0. [INFERRED] One hundred thousand 1,024-dimension vectors take about 205 MB in fp16, so a NumPy matrix plus an exact boolean filter (which handles the existing excluded-documents list) beats approximate indexes. [PROPOSED] Store one `vectors_<model>.npy` file per model under test plus one SQLite file holding passage text, metadata and an FTS5 table, with the model revision recorded in the index header.

### Check the GPU before tuning anything

[DIRECT] PyPI's torch 2.14.1 wheel for Windows and Python 3.14 is about 124 MB and CPU-only; the PyTorch CUDA 13.0 index carries a `torch-2.14.1+cu130` wheel for Python 3.14 on Windows ([PyTorch cu130 index](https://download.pytorch.org/whl/cu130/torch/)); CUDA 13.x applications need NVIDIA driver 580 or newer ([CUDA release notes](https://docs.nvidia.com/cuda/cuda-toolkit-release-notes/index.html)); and CUDA 12.8 was the first toolkit to generate native Blackwell code ([Blackwell guide](https://docs.nvidia.com/cuda/blackwell-compatibility-guide/index.html)). torchvision 0.29.1 excludes exactly Python 3.14.1 ([PyPI torchvision](https://pypi.org/pypi/torchvision/json)); sentence-transformers 6.1.0 caps huggingface-hub below 2.0; outlines and infinity-emb exclude 3.14; flash-attn has no Windows wheels; onnxruntime 1.31.0 and OpenVINO 2026.4.1 ship Python 3.14 Windows wheels; and `mteb` requires Python below 3.15 ([PyPI JSON for each](https://pypi.org/pypi/onnxruntime/json)). [INFERRED] The current 10–30 s bge-m3 load and the 40 s search over 13 cases may simply be CPU execution from the PyPI wheel. [PROPOSED] Log `torch.__version__`, `torch.version.cuda`, `torch.cuda.is_available()` and `torch.cuda.get_arch_list()`, run `python --version` to rule out 3.14.1, and keep one virtual environment per model family because some libraries (PyLate, for example) pin old sentence-transformers versions.

[DIRECT] Sentence-transformers can run embedders on CPU through ONNX or OpenVINO; in its benchmarks, which included bge-m3, ONNX int8 was 3.08× faster than fp32 PyTorch on short texts, but ONNX "can even perform slightly worse" on long texts ([sentence-transformers efficiency](https://sbert.net/docs/sentence_transformer/usage/efficiency.html)). [INFERRED] An embedder plus a reranker resident on the GPU take about 2.3–2.4 GB of weights in fp16, leaving roughly 5.5 GB for a local writer; queries are short, so moving retrieval models to the CPU while a local writer is active costs little, provided the corpus is embedded once and cached. Changing runtime or precision changes the vectors, so the corpus must be re-embedded and the 129/359 ceiling re-measured before int8 is adopted.

[PROPOSED] The fair experiment runs in stages on fixed chunks: freeze the environment and model revisions; make the statement the unit and split statements about 60/40 into development and sealed test sets, stratified by topic and by source, with WHO statements in both; freeze query templates; take coverage@10 on development as the primary metric with the depth curve (6, 20, 50, 100) as the ceiling; compare, in order, bge-m3's modes and BM25 tokenisers, then embedders, then rerankers, then query variants, then a per-document cap and final k (about 8–12, chosen against the writer's token cost); pick the simplest configuration within the bootstrap confidence interval of the best; and run it once on the sealed test set, with paired cluster bootstrap over statements and at most about ten configurations (or a Holm correction). An optional secondary set of about 20 Arabic topic queries, translated by an Arabic-reading prosthetist, compares cross-lingual behaviour.

### Settings that protect verbatim quoting

[DIRECT] Explicit chain-of-thought "can significantly degrade instruction-following accuracy" across 15 models ([When Thinking Fails](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.11423)), and in grounded summarisation a reasoning model hallucinated 14.3% against 3.9% for its non-reasoning sibling ([Vectara blog](https://vectara.com/blog/deepseek-r1-hallucinates-more-than-deepseek-v3)). Qwen3.5 has thinking on by default, switched off with `chat_template_kwargs: {"enable_thinking": False}`, and Qwen recommends a presence penalty of 1.5 for its non-thinking mode ([Qwen3.5-9B card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.5-9B)); Gemma 4 thinks only when `<|think|>` opens the system prompt ([Gemma 4 E4B card](https://huggingface.co/google/gemma-4-E4B-it)). Schema-constrained decoding did not hurt task accuracy in the best controlled test (llama.cpp's grammar raised GSM8K from 80.1 to 82.4 and compliance on hard schemas from 0.13 to 0.39), although llama.cpp failed to compile 37 categories of the JSON-Schema test suite ([JSONSchemaBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.10868v3)); the claim that format restrictions hurt reasoning ([Tam et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.02442)) has a credible rebuttal ([.txt](https://blog.dottxt.ai/say-what-you-mean.html)). 8-bit quantisation costs about 0.8% on long-context tasks while 4-bit methods lost up to 59%, with worse results for non-English input ([long-context quantisation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.20276v2)), and human raters saw far larger multilingual losses than automatic metrics did (Japanese −16.0% vs −1.7%) ([multilingual quantisation](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.03211v2)). [PROPOSED] Run the writer with thinking off, at temperature 0 plus one run at the vendor's non-thinking settings, with neutral presence and repetition penalties (a penalty may push the model away from copying passage text), JSON constrained to a simple schema (object, required, enum, string, array; counts such as "1 per gap field, 4 per section" enforced in code; no `$ref`), fields ordered topic → passage id → quote → English → Arabic, and Q5_K_M or Q6_K weights where memory allows, with Q4_K_M as the floor and Arabic quality checked at each level.

[DIRECT] Selecting source spans before generating keeps or improves attribution and cuts verification time ([Attribute First, then Generate](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.17104)); a small quote-first extractor beat full-context fine-tuning by more than 20 points ([LLMQuoter](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.05554)); and a 150M-parameter span extractor beat the best LLM extractor on word-level F1 (53.6 vs 48.7), partly because LLMs pulled evidence from irrelevant chunks ([ACL-Verbatim summary](https://hyper.ai/en/papers/2605.21102)). [PROPOSED] One option, which changes the writer contract and is therefore the owner's decision, is to have the model return a passage id plus span indices instead of retyping the quote, so that code cuts the exact text and misquotes become impossible by construction; test it as an ablation before adopting it.

[DIRECT] QLoRA of an 8B model needs about 6 GB on Unsloth, which supports Windows and RTX 50 cards but trains only on Python 3.11–3.13 ([Unsloth](https://unsloth.ai/docs/get-started/fine-tuning-for-beginners/unsloth-requirements)). [INFERRED] Fine-tuning is not justified now: the 59 statements and 13 golden cases are the test set, retrieval limits coverage far more than the writer does, and English-heavy training risks Arabic quality. If systematic writer failures remain after the protocol below, the evidence-backed target is a quote-first span extractor on an Apache-2.0 base of 9B or less, trained without the golden cases. [UNKNOWN] Whether API providers allow their outputs to be used to train another model was not checked.

[PROPOSED] The model test freezes retrieval once per golden case so every candidate sees byte-identical prompts, scores two input sets (the real top-6 retrieval, and only the topics whose passages contain answer-key statements, which is the fair test of a writer), splits cases into development and held-out sets, and runs three repeats per case. Hard gates come first: 100% valid JSON with constraints, zero confirmed advice or ranking items, no out-of-memory error at the longest prompt, and peak VRAM within an owner-set margin such as 7 GB. Survivors are ranked on coverage of the retrievable set, quote-check pass rate with a histogram of removal reasons, English faithfulness scored by HHEM-2.1-Open with a human check of the 20 lowest items, a blind 0–2 human rating of about 40 Arabic sentences per configuration plus a glossary-adherence rate, and time per case; only the top two get ablations (Q4 against Q5/Q6, f16 against q8_0 KV, thinking off against a short budget, constrained against unconstrained JSON, and one call against a separate Arabic step).


## 5. Code, not the model, must decide what is supported and when to abstain

[DIRECT] The prototype's `find_quote` proves that a quote exists in its passage, "not that the AI's sentence says only what the quote says" ([handoff](/root/.claude/uploads/830110ea-030d-5d0c-9ab6-615062c1589f/85794c40-handoff_prompt_2026-10-11.md)). That gap is where the documented risk lives: across seven LLMs and 58,000 statement–source pairs, "between 50% and 90% of LLM responses are not fully supported" by their cited sources, and even GPT-4o with web search left about 30% of individual statements unsupported ([SourceCheckup, Nature Communications 2025](https://law.stanford.edu/publications/an-automated-framework-for-assessing-how-well-llms-cite-relevant-medical-references/)). In clinical notes, hallucinations split into fabrication (43%), negation (30%), contextual (17%) and causality (10%) errors; structured prompts cut major hallucinations by 75% and function calls cut major omissions from 61 to 0, but "atomising" the task raised major hallucinations from 4 to 25, so every workflow change has to be measured (preprint) ([CREOLA](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.09.12.24313556.full.pdf)).

[DIRECT] Small grounding checkers now match GPT-4 on the LLM-AggreFact benchmark and run locally, but their open weights are English-only. Bespoke-MiniCheck-7B averages 77.4 but is CC BY-NC 4.0; Granite Guardian 3.3 8B scores 76.5 under Apache-2.0, English only; MiniCheck-Flan-T5-L (0.8B) scores 75.0 with an unverified weight licence ([LLM-AggreFact](https://llm-aggrefact.github.io/)). HHEM-2.1-Open is Apache-2.0, about 0.1B parameters, needs under 600 MB of RAM and takes about 1.5 s per 2,000-token input on a CPU, with balanced accuracy 76.55% against GPT-4's 73.78% on one benchmark; its cross-lingual successor is available only on Vectara's platform ([HHEM card](https://huggingface.co/vectara/hallucination_evaluation_model)). A multilingual natural-language-inference model scores 0.794 on Arabic against 0.871 on English ([mDeBERTa XNLI](https://huggingface.co/MoritzLaurer/mDeBERTa-v3-base-xnli-multilingual-nli-2mil7)), and even a fine-tuned GPT-3.5 reached only about 80% macro-F1 on attribution judgements ([AttributionBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.15089)). [UNKNOWN] No grounding benchmark on prosthetics, orthotics or rehabilitation text and no Arabic clinical claim-verification benchmark were found.

[PROPOSED] Verification should therefore be a cascade, with every rejection logged with its reason, as `removed` already does:

| Step | Check | Fails when | Tooling |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Quote exists | Normalised quote not in the passage (add Arabic normalisation before any Arabic source is admitted) | Existing `find_quote`; §8 normaliser |
| 2 | Deterministic fidelity | A number, unit, range, negation (not, no, never, without; لا، لم، ليس، دون), laterality, amputation level, K-level or modal-strength word in the English sentence is absent from the quote; Arabic numbers differ from English ones | Code |
| 3 | Entailment | English sentence not entailed by its quote (optionally plus one neighbouring sentence) above a calibrated threshold | HHEM-2.1-Open on CPU; Granite Guardian 3.3 8B in 4-bit as a test |
| 4 | Policy | Ranking, recommending, diagnosing, prescribing or "ready for fitting" wording, in either language | Pattern rules plus, later, a classifier |
| 5 | Arabic | Arabic sentence breaks the glossary or critical-token checks (§8) | Code; every Arabic sentence reaches a human reviewer |
| 6 | Second opinion (optional) | Sampled items flagged by a judge from a different provider than the writer | Triage only |

[INFERRED] Because the best checkers average only about 75–77 on hard benchmarks, a checker cannot be the sole safeguard: its threshold must be calibrated on a project set of faithful restatements of answer-key quotes plus generated hard negatives (an altered number, a flipped negation, a swapped side, an overgeneralised scope), and its own precision and recall reported. The step 2 rules are a design proposal; no published evaluation of rule-based numeric-fidelity checks was found. Because citations raise users' trust "even when the citations were random" ([Ding et al., AAAI 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.01303)), passing the quote check must never be shown as "verified correct": the quote is the evidence and the sentence is a summary to check.

[DIRECT] Models are poor at abstaining on their own. On MetaMedQA, **9 of 12 models scored 0%** on unanswerable questions and GPT-4o scored 3.7%, although telling GPT-4o that some questions may be unanswerable improved it significantly ([Griot et al., Nature Communications 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-024-55628-6)); reasoning fine-tuning degraded abstention by 24% on average across 20 models (preprint) ([AbstentionBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.09038)); and adding insufficient context raised one model's wrong-answer rate from 10.2% to 66.1%, while an LLM autorater judged context sufficiency correctly at least 93% of the time ([Sufficient Context, ICLR 2025](https://research.google/blog/deeper-insights-into-retrieval-augmented-generation-the-role-of-sufficient-context/)). [PROPOSED] A topic therefore receives "No sufficient evidence identified in the indexed sources" when code finds that no passage passes a retrieval threshold calibrated on the answer key, a sufficiency check marks the passages insufficient, verification removed every candidate item, or the source registry holds no source of an allowed evidence layer for that domain. Each abstention shows what was searched (sources and versions, the query, top scores, the date), which frames it as absence of evidence in the index rather than evidence of absence. When passages disagree, both sides are shown with their layers and the conflict is never resolved by the model. Evaluation labels each (case × topic) as answerable or unanswerable, adds probes with the key source removed and out-of-scope questions, and reports abstention precision and recall plus a risk–coverage curve. [INFERRED] The 36% retrieval ceiling means many topics should currently abstain, which makes this testable at once. [UNKNOWN] No abstention study in prosthetics or rehabilitation and no validated bge-m3 sufficiency threshold for this corpus exist.

### The review page must make checking easier than trusting

[DIRECT] Wrong AI suggestions pull trained clinicians toward errors: in a randomised trial, 44 physicians who had all completed 20 hours of AI-literacy training scored **14.0 points lower (95% CI −18.9 to −9.1)** on diagnostic reasoning when ChatGPT-4o advice contained deliberate errors in three of six vignettes (preprint) ([medRxiv RCT](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.08.23.25334280.full.pdf)); in another trial, GPT-4 access did not improve physicians' reasoning (76% vs 74%) ([Goh et al., JAMA Netw Open 2024](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2825395)); and in rehabilitation, five therapists using an LLM improved overall but changed about three right answers to wrong ([Frontiers in Digital Health 2026](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/digital-health/articles/10.3389/fdgth.2025.1719340/full)). First-person uncertainty phrasing ("I'm not sure, but…") reduced over-reliance in a pre-registered study of 404 people ([Kim et al., FAccT 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.00623)). The FDA's January 2026 decision-support guidance asks that a professional be able to "independently review the basis", including sources "identified with dates or versions" and "knowns and unknowns" such as missing inputs, names automation bias as a risk, and excludes software for critical, time-sensitive decisions ([FDA CDS guidance](https://www.fda.gov/media/109618/download)). [PROPOSED] The review page should put the source's exact words first and the AI sentence second, labelled as a summary; take evidence-layer, source, publisher, year and page badges from the source registry, never from the model; collapse experimental-layer items into a section labelled "research only, not clinical guidance"; show a "not found" panel with the search trace and a missing-information panel driven by the intake schema; place conflicts side by side without adjudication; show a per-item verification status, with first-person cautionary wording for uncertain items; require an explicit approve, edit or reject decision per item with reason codes, never pre-selecting "approve" and never showing "recommended", ranks or totals; log whether the reviewer opened the passage in context; and keep a bilingual disclaimer that the specialist decides. In the specialist trial, with consent and a debrief, a few seeded flawed items (a wrong number, an overgeneralisation, a wrong side) give the only direct measure of automation bias for this system. [UNKNOWN] No study has tested quote-first layouts or layer badges on clinicians.

[DIRECT] Two further failure modes enter through inputs. In a news report of an npj Digital Medicine study, GPT models complied with illogical medical requests 100% of the time, and permission to reject plus prompting to recall facts raised rejection to 94% ([Euronews](https://www.euronews.com/2025/10/17/ai-models-bias-toward-flattery-risks-spreading-false-medical-information-study-warns)); all four models tested were susceptible to prompt injection, including instructions hidden in images ([Clusmann et al., Nature Communications 2025](https://ideas.repec.org/a/nat/natcom/v16y2025i1d10.1038_s41467-024-55631-x.html)). [INFERRED] Sycophancy can arrive through the intake form ("patient is K4, confirm components"), and injection risk is low with a curated local corpus but rises sharply with user uploads or web sources. [PROPOSED] Keep user opinions out of the prompt where possible, instruct the model to report conflicts between case data and sources, delimit retrieved passages as data, hash-verify corpus files against the manifest, and test false-premise and injected documents in a test-only index.

### Keep the workflow fixed and the records provable

[PROPOSED] The clinical path stays a fixed pipeline in plain Python with Pydantic models: a deterministic router to the domain pack, a topic planner, parallel retrieval per topic, the writer per part, the verification cascade, abstention and conflict decisions, then the review page. Agents are acceptable only offline and behind human gates (drafting source metadata, proposing rules for the owner's approval, generating hard-negative test pairs). [DIRECT] All the major frameworks are actively released, including LangGraph 1.2.15, LlamaIndex core 0.14.25, Haystack 3.3.0, DSPy 3.4.1 and Pydantic AI 2.55.0 ([PyPI langgraph](https://pypi.org/project/langgraph/); [PyPI dspy](https://pypi.org/project/dspy/)), but no head-to-head evidence on their reliability for clinical RAG was found. [INFERRED] None is needed for a fixed three-part pipeline; DSPy could later tune prompts against the answer key, but only with a frozen held-out split.

[DIRECT] SQLite's own guidance says that "for device-local storage with low writer concurrency and less than a terabyte of content, SQLite is almost always a better solution", and that a client–server database fits many computers writing over a network ([sqlite.org](https://www.sqlite.org/whentouse.html)); W3C PROV defines provenance as information about the entities, activities and people involved in producing data ([W3C PROV](https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-overview/)), and FHIR R5 Provenance is based on it, with entity roles such as *quotation* and *source* ([FHIR Provenance](https://hl7.org/fhir/R5/provenance.html)). TRIPOD-LLM asks studies to report the model name, version and date and whether the model was frozen during data collection ([TRIPOD-LLM](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-024-03425-5)). [PROPOSED] Move from JSON, JSONL and CSV files to one SQLite file with tables for sources, passages, knowledge items, cases, runs, generated items (kept and removed) and decisions; make `decisions` append-only with triggers that reject UPDATE and DELETE and a SHA-256 hash chain that makes tampering detectable; keep a JSONL export for transparency; and store a run manifest with every generation (git commit, domain-pack version and hash, prompt version and prompt hash, writer backend and exact model id, corpus and index hashes, retriever settings with each topic's passage ids and scores, checker version and thresholds, timestamps, tokens and cost). Because hosted and local outputs cannot be reproduced bit for bit, "replay" re-runs checks and the interface on stored outputs instead of regenerating, and golden-case results hold only for the model snapshot named in the manifest. PostgreSQL with pgvector is warranted only if several specialists review concurrently over a network.

[DIRECT] Evidence on AI coding productivity is weak and mixed: experienced developers were 19% slower with AI while believing they were faster ([METR RCT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.09089)), METR's 2026 follow-up called its data "only very weak evidence" of improvement ([METR update](https://metr.org/blog/2026-02-24-uplift-update/)), and DORA calls AI "an amplifier" of existing practice ([DORA 2025](https://dora.dev/research/2025/dora-report/)). [PROPOSED] The owner's gated workflow already matches the best guidance; each phase should add a short written spec, tests and golden expectations written before code and approved by the owner, small agent-written diffs, an independent review session against the spec, `verify_all.py` plus the evaluation gate, an explain-back so the learning developer understands every merged change, and only then a commit. The yardstick (answer-key files, golden expectations, the evidence-layer table, the review log) should be writable only with the owner's approval, enforced by a Claude Code hook or a pre-commit check, so that an agent cannot "fix" a failing evaluation by changing the expected answer ([Claude Code best practices](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/best-practices)).


## 6. Prosthetics evidence: no strong recommendations and no tested readiness criteria

[DIRECT] The anchor is the VA/DoD guideline for rehabilitation after lower-limb amputation, version 3.0 (January 2025, literature to 15 March 2024): **26 recommendations, 0 strong, 16 "weak for" and 10 "neither for nor against"**. It supersedes the 2017 version, says it is not a standard of care, and does not use the term "K-level" ([provider summary](https://www.healthquality.va.gov/guidelines/Rehab/amp/LLA-CPG_2024-Provider-Summary_final_20250110.pdf); [full guideline](https://www.healthquality.va.gov/HEALTHQUALITY/guidelines/Rehab/amp/LLA-CPG_2024-Guideline_final_20250110.pdf)). A clinician's guide dates the release to December 2024, a small conflict with the January 2025 documents ([USUHS record](https://scholar.usuhs.edu/en/publications/clinicians-guide-to-the-2024-vadod-clinical-practice-guideline-fo/)). The VA page, updated 9 July 2026, also links six 2025 clinical support tools: Routine Care, Living Well with Limb Loss, Residual Limb Management, Gait Analysis, Pain Management, and Pregnancy and Limb Loss ([VA LLA page](https://www.healthquality.va.gov/guidelines/Rehab/amp/index.asp)). The upper-limb guideline, version 2.0 (March 2022), has 14 recommendations (4 weak for, 10 neither); its weak recommendation for a body-powered *or* externally powered prosthesis rests on one observational telephone survey of 755 people, with "very low" confidence, and it found "no evidence ... to recommend one type of prosthetic system over another" ([ULA full guideline](https://healthquality.va.gov/HEALTHQUALITY/guidelines/Rehab/ULA/VADoDULACPG_Final_508.pdf)); a secondary summary reports 18 recommendations, which conflicts with the primary document ([Centre for Evidence](https://www.centreforevidence.org/article/20230301-management-of-upper-limb-amputation-rehabilitation-synopsis-of-the-2022-us-department-of-veterans-affairs-and-us-department-of-defense-clinical-practice-guideline-for-acquired)). The 2020 Dutch evidence-based guideline used AGREE II and GRADE over 59 studies searched to March 2019 and is © ISPO ([Dutch guideline PDF](https://cris.maastrichtuniversity.nl/ws/files/114567660/Daemen_2022_Amputation_and_prosthetics_of_the_lower_extremity.pdf)). NICE's osseointegration guidance IPG795 has been migrated to HealthTech guidance HTG732 with its "special arrangements" recommendation unchanged ([NICE HTG732](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/htg732)).

[DIRECT] The professional guidelines from Hanger Institute authors are ungraded or consensus-based and now have confirmed licences: the foot guideline (2018; four ungraded recommendations; CC BY-NC-ND) ([PMC6221375](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6221375)), the knee guideline (online 2018; CC BY-NC-ND) ([OpenAlex](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1097/JPO.0000000000000214)), the transtibial socket, interface and suspension guideline (online 2018; six recommendations; CC BY-NC-ND; DOI not shown) ([Hanger PDF](https://hangerclinic.com/wp-content/uploads/clinical-practice-guidelines-transtibial-socket.pdf)) and the transradial consensus (2021, not 2022 as a company page said; CC BY-NC; 22 experts agreed on 40 of 55 statements "in the absence of conclusive empirical evidence") ([OpenAlex](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1177/20556683211065262)). The 2016 items that one research track listed as "CPGs published in *Arch PM&R*" are conference abstracts ([Hanger abstract](https://hangerclinic.com/wp-content/uploads/clinical-practice-guidelines-postop-care-abstract.pdf)).

| Topic | VA/DoD 2025 (native grade) | Other appraised sources |
|---|---|---|
| Rigid or semi-rigid post-operative dressing (R5) | Weak for; very low confidence; no new evidence found | Cochrane 2019: very low, "uncertain"; Dutch 2020: "consider", little or no difference in time to prosthesis (low) |
| Transfemoral socket design (R15–R16) | Neither, including ischial containment vs subischial | Dutch 2020: "consider" subischial for volume-stable limbs of sufficient length (very low, expert-based) |

[INFERRED] Any statement drawn from these sources must carry its grade ("weak recommendation", "insufficient evidence"); "neither" is an insufficiency statement to display, not to drop; and where a guideline says "neither", the system must not fill the gap with textbook or manufacturer text presented as evidence. The industry-affiliated guidelines should carry that flag and never share the visual weight of GRADE recommendations.

### Components: direction is consistent, certainty is low

[DIRECT] For microprocessor knees, a 2014 review of six publications (57 limited community ambulators) found they may "reduce uncontrolled falls by up to 80%" ([Kannenberg et al.](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1682/JRRD.2014.05.0118)), a 2022 review of 18 articles including 7 RCTs found better function and quality of life without GRADE ([Thibaut et al.](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.23736/S1973-9087.22.07238-0)), and the ASCENT K2 trial randomised 107 above-knee users over 65 (mostly dysvascular) and found fewer falls (p = 0.015) and near-falls (p = 0.001) with a microprocessor knee over 12 months, with Otto Bock-affiliated co-authors ([ASCENT K2, CC BY](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1080/09638288.2025.2530172)); a within-subject trial of 50 low-mobility users pointed the same way ([FASTK2](https://mayoclinic.elsevierpure.com/en/publications/functional-assessment-and-satisfaction-of-transfemoral-amputees-w)). The US AHRQ review found no participant characteristics that predict who benefits from a component (low strength of evidence), did not compare components' overall effectiveness, excluded biomechanical outcomes, and reported that 11–22% stop using their prosthesis at about one year, with transfemoral users about twice as likely to stop as transtibial users ([AHRQ CER 213](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK531523/)). [INFERRED] ASCENT K2 post-dates the VA/DoD evidence cut-off and involves industry, so it is "emerging" evidence that agrees in direction with R17 but must not be shown as changing the guideline's grade.

[DIRECT] For feet, an industry-authored mini-review of 38 publications found low-to-moderate, mostly gait-laboratory evidence: powered feet "may be considered" for high-functioning users, but "it remains unclear to what extent the gait lab results" carry over to daily life ([Kannenberg, CPOJ 2018, CC BY](https://distantreader.org/stacks/journals/cpoj/cpoj-30450.pdf)). For partial-foot amputation, 29 high-risk-of-bias articles suggest mobility and quality of life "may be similar" to transtibial amputation, with frequent delayed healing and re-amputation ([Dillon et al. 2017](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13643-017-0433-7)). For hip disarticulation, transpelvic and bilateral levels only case series and narrative reviews were found ([Moura & Garruço](https://doaj.org/article/dd487c8ab9de46b1bfa91696d211765c)), so the correct output is "No sufficient evidence identified". For the upper limb, a review of 31 studies found body-powered devices better on durability, training time, maintenance and feedback and myoelectric devices better on appearance and phantom pain, but evidence "insufficient to conclude" an overall advantage ([Carey et al. 2015](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1682/JRRD.2014.08.0192)); pattern-recognition control has one three-person home trial ([Kuiken et al.](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1109/JTEHM.2016.2616123)); and rejection reached 44% in 68 Austrian traumatic upper-limb amputees, unchanged after 2006 (p = 0.939), with comfort and weight the leading complaints ([Salminger et al.](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1080/09638288.2020.1866684)).

### Sockets, suspension, liners and alignment: the weakest topics are also the least evidenced

[DIRECT] No graded guideline makes a positive recommendation for any socket design. For transfemoral sockets, a review of 13 low-quality clinical studies found ischial containment the most studied design but not superior ([Brodie et al. 2021](https://strathprints.strath.ac.uk/79766)), and the newest evidence, a multicentre randomised crossover trial in 25 users from 10 French centres, found better Socket Comfort Scores with a subischial suction socket (+1.8, p = 0.008, d = 0.70) but no significant difference in mobility or two-minute walk distance; 18 of 22 users (82%) kept the subischial socket, only three participants were women, the abstract and body report 23 and 22 completers, and three authors are employed by the sponsor ([Loiret et al. 2026, CC BY](https://www.mdpi.com/2673-1592/8/1/5)). For transtibial sockets, the Hanger guideline states that "Total surface bearing sockets are indicated to decrease fitting times and enable higher activity levels", that "vacuum-assisted suspension (VAS) sockets permit the least amount of pistoning", and that VAS sockets "require both awareness and compliance ... and are not universally indicated", while conceding that "deficits in the available literature preclude CPGs ... from providing comprehensive clinical guidance" ([Stevens et al.](https://hangerclinic.com/wp-content/uploads/clinical-practice-guidelines-transtibial-socket.pdf)). A two-part review of 35 articles found a trend toward better gait symmetry, volume control and limb health with vacuum-assisted sockets, in studies of "low to moderate methodological rigor" ([Safari & Meier, part 1](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1682/JRRD.2014.08.0183); [O&P EDGE summary of part 2](https://opedge.com/?p=127793)), a 2019 review found that "PTB sockets are still successfully used and in some studies preferred over TSB" ([Al Ahuaili et al.](https://strathprints.strath.ac.uk/70565)), and the 1992 Atlas calls the PTB socket "appropriate for virtually all" transtibial amputations ([Atlas ch. 18B](https://oandplibrary.org/alp/chap18-02.asp)). For suspension, a review of elevated-vacuum studies (mean samples of 7 transtibial and 6 transfemoral users) concluded it "may not be appropriate for all people with limb loss" ([Gholizadeh et al. 2016](https://pesquisa.bvsalud.org/ses/resource/es/mdl-27423025)), and an earlier review, read only through trade press, found pin-lock total-surface-bearing systems favoured by most users with "insufficient clinical evidence" for a standard ([O&P EDGE](https://opedge.com/news_2013-12-19_02/)). For liners, "there is insufficient research to differentiate" user experience ([Richardson & Dillon](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1177/0309364616631343)), and the Dutch guideline advises against liners marketed with "magnetic shielding" for phantom pain. [INFERRED] PTB against TSB is mainly an outdated-versus-current difference confounded by suspension and liner, and pistoning against user preference compares different outcomes, so both belong side by side rather than as contradictions. Suspension remains the weakest topic: no freely quotable systematic review was found.

[DIRECT] Alignment is still optimised "through repetitive optical gait observation" in a "trial-and-error process"; alignment changes "have a predictable influence on the included kinetic parameters", while effects on spatio-temporal and kinematic parameters "are generally unpredictable" ([Jonkergouw et al. 2016, CC BY](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0167466)). A 2009 state-of-the-science review produced 113 evidence statements, 43 of them high or moderate confidence, but its publisher forbids archiving ([Neumann 2009](https://oasis.library.unlv.edu/fac_articles/228)); coronal misalignment systematically changes socket moments in 11 users ([Boone et al. 2013](https://research.polyu.edu.hk/en/publications/influence-of-malalignment-on-socket-reaction-moments-during-gait-/)); and guiding alignment by socket moments was feasible in 10 users, though "a sole reliance ... might be too simple" ([Jonkergouw et al. 2019](https://strathprints.strath.ac.uk/66186/)). The VA/DoD gait tool pairs about 22 deviations with patient and prosthetic causes without references or grades, warning "Analyze the prosthesis, but do not ignore the patient!" ([VA/DoD Gait Analysis](https://www.healthquality.va.gov/HEALTHQUALITY/guidelines/Rehab/amp/LLA-CST-03-04182025-Gait-Analysis-Final-Draft-508.pdf)); its table columns were scrambled in extraction and must be re-extracted. [INFERRED] No validated optimal alignment criterion exists, deviation-to-cause tables are expert hypotheses to flag for assessment, and the system must never output alignment settings.

[DIRECT] For residual-limb volume, measurement techniques "lack adequate resolution", and the evidence focuses on early post-operative transtibial adults ([Sanders & Fatone 2011](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4423813/)); only water displacement and circumferential methods proved reliable, in transtibial limbs only ([Armitage et al. 2019](https://researchers.mq.edu.au/en/publications/reliability-and-validity-of-measurement-tools-for-residual-limb-v/)); a critically appraised topic found doffing the prosthesis and liner for a recovery period the most effective strategy across five small studies ([Childers CAT](https://assets.noviams.com/novi-file-uploads/aaop/CATs/CAT_groff-embry-childers.pdf)); adjustable sockets rest on small studies ([Baldock et al. 2023](https://doaj.org/article/18fdc1105143473388bf5800355cba30)); and the VA/DoD residual-limb tool gives practical rules such as "Limit wear time if redness does not resolve in 15-20 min" and shrinker use whenever the prosthesis is off ([VA/DoD Residual Limb Management](https://www.healthquality.va.gov/HEALTHQUALITY/guidelines/Rehab/amp/LLA-CST-02-04152025-Residual-Limb-Management-Final-Draft-508.pdf)).

### Readiness for fitting: prerequisites and predictors, never a verdict

[DIRECT] No source verified in this research defines or tests readiness criteria against outcomes. Rigid dressings may shorten healing (mean difference −25.60 days in one study of 56), but every outcome in the 2019 Cochrane review is very low certainty and "prosthetic readiness" is "unclear" ([Cochrane CD012427](https://www.cochrane.org/evidence/CD012427_rigid-versus-soft-dressings-transtibial-below-knee-amputations)). Timing comes from expert texts 34 years apart: preparatory fitting "usually 6 to 10 weeks postoperatively" and a definitive prosthesis after "12 to 18 months" ([Merck Manual 2026](https://www.merckmanuals.com/professional/special-subjects/limb-prosthetics/preparing-for-a-limb-prosthesis)), against atrophic changes that "may stabilize after only 4 months" and a definitive-fitting decision that is "largely subjective" ([Atlas ch. 18B](https://oandplibrary.org/alp/chap18-02.asp)). The upper-limb guideline requires the patient to be "medically, surgically, and cognitively cleared by the care team for a diagnostic socket fitting to occur" ([ULA full guideline](https://healthquality.va.gov/HEALTHQUALITY/guidelines/Rehab/ULA/VADoDULACPG_Final_508.pdf)). Prognostic reviews associate good walking with cognition, fitness, one-leg standing, independence in daily activities and pre-operative mobility, and poor walking with longer time from surgery to rehabilitation and stump problems ([Sansam et al. 2009](https://medicaljournalssweden.se/jrm/article/view/18086)); a 2016 update of 21 studies named aetiology, fitness, living status, level, age and comorbidity as "moderate to strongly supported" predictors but gave no clear rule for candidacy ([update](https://www.ingentaconnect.com/content/nai/ti/2016/00000018/f0020002/art00005)). [INFERRED] These are associations, not criteria: longer time to rehabilitation may reflect illness severity, so it must never be rendered as "early fitting improves outcome", and the dressing sources form a conflict group that agrees in direction and differs in the strength of its wording. [PROPOSED] Use case 01 should list which readiness dimensions are undocumented (wound, shape and volume stability, skin, pain, cognition, cardiovascular fitness) and show what sources associate with outcome or what experts describe as prerequisites, never a readiness score and never "ready".

### K-levels are US coverage policy, and populations are skewed

[DIRECT] Medicare's Local Coverage Determination L33787 defines functional levels 0–4 on the "reasonable expectations" of the practitioner and prosthetist, requires records to document current capability and expected potential, and carries AMA copyright on its CPT content; one research track read these points in the current version (effective 1 April 2026), while the code-to-level mapping (for example SACH feet from Level 1 and microprocessor knees L5856–L5858 from Level 3 with Level 2 exceptions) and the "never covered" list (L5969, L5990) were read in the version effective 1 October 2025 to 31 March 2026, which version 40 has since superseded ([CMS LCD L33787](https://www.cms.gov/medicare-coverage-database/view/lcd.aspx?lcdid=33787)). The Amputee Mobility Predictor is reliable (test–retest ICC 0.96 and 0.88; MDC90 3.4 points) and discriminated K-levels in its development study, but a retrospective study of 123 people found it did not separate K3 from K4 ([SRALab AMP](https://www.sralab.org/rehabilitation-measures/amputee-mobility-predictor)). [INFERRED] The K-level is a payer construct, not a clinical classification validated by guideline evidence. [PROPOSED] Label all K-level text "Medicare coverage policy (US)" with the LCD version, never assign a K-level, keep trial evidence (ASCENT K2) separate from the coverage rule, and check the handoff's open item on `cms_power_assist_ankle` (L5969) against version 40, since the version read lists L5969 as never covered.

[DIRECT] Women made up 25.4% of participants in five years of prosthetics journal studies against about 34.6% of people with amputation (a trade-press summary of a 2024 review) ([O&P EDGE](https://opedge.com/review-shows-gender-disparity-in-lower-limb-prosthetic-research/)); older dysvascular transfemoral users are covered by one crossover trial of 10 people ([Cochrane CD005260](https://www.cochranelibrary.com/cdsr/doi/10.1002/14651858.CD005260.pub4/pdf/CDSR/CD005260/CD005260_abstract.pdf)); and Humanity & Inclusion reports about 3 million people injured and 86,000 amputations in Syria ([HI 2025](https://www.humanity-inclusion.org.uk/en/amputation-crisis-in-conflict-zones-report-reveals-urgent-need-for-rehabilitation-services-in-gaza-syria-and-ukraine)). [INFERRED] Evidence-population bias and retrieval bias compound here: the low-resource-setting manuals most relevant to Syrian users are exactly the sources retrieval misses (the WHO manual never ranks, and the ICRC manual is excluded from gap search), so population tags and per-case mismatch warnings are core safety features (§10, §13).


## 7. Orthotics, gait and rehabilitation each need their own gates

### Orthotics: five graded guideline areas and mostly low-certainty reviews

[DIRECT] Graded guidelines were verified in five orthotic areas. For stroke, the ANPT guideline (122 studies to November 2019, adults, acute under 3 months and chronic from 3 months) grades AFO or FES for gait speed at evidence level I, strong ("should provide"; chronic AFO and FES each I/strong, acute AFO I/moderate and FES II/moderate), adds mobility, balance and endurance statements, and says an AFO or FES "should not" be provided when reducing plantarflexor spasticity is the primary goal (II/moderate); it found neither device superior, judged AFO effects compensatory and FES effects therapeutic, could not compare AFO types, and marks its tables "© 2018 APTA. All rights reserved" ([PEDro record](https://search.pedro.org.au/search-results/record-detail/65992); [JNPT](https://journals.lww.com/jnpt/Fulltext/2021/04000/A_Clinical_Practice_Guideline_for_the_Use_of.6.aspx)); the per-phase grades were transcribed from its Table 3 and must be re-checked. NICE HTG178 judges the evidence on FES for central drop foot "adequate ... provided that normal arrangements are in place", with surface FES adding 0.18 m/s in three controlled studies of 71 stroke patients, on evidence from 2009 ([NICE HTG178](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/htg178/resources/functional-electrical-stimulation-for-drop-foot-of-central-neurological-origin-pdf-1809587890741957)). For the diabetic foot, IWGDF 2023 says to "use a non-removable knee-high offloading device as the first choice" for neuropathic plantar forefoot or midfoot ulcers (strong, moderate certainty), not to use standard therapeutic footwear instead of an offloading device (strong, low), and adjusts for infection and ischaemia ([IWGDF offloading](https://iwgdfguidelines.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/07/IWGDF-2023-06-Offloading-Guideline.pdf)); its prevention guideline says to "prescribe therapeutic footwear that has a demonstrated plantar pressure relieving effect during walking" after a healed plantar ulcer (strong, moderate) and sets risk categories 0–3 with screening from yearly to every 1–3 months ([PubMed 37302121](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37302121/); read from a third-party copy, to be re-fetched from IWGDF). For scoliosis, SOSORT 2016, now confirmed CC BY 4.0, states "Bracing is recommended to treat adolescent idiopathic scoliosis" (SoRT B, evidence I), defines night (8–12 h), part-time (12–20 h) and full-time (20–24 h) rigid bracing, and calls in-brace correction "a preliminary indicator" ([SOSORT](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1186/s13013-017-0145-8.pdf)), while Cochrane rates the evidence that bracing slows progression low to very low ([Cochrane CD006850](https://www.cochranelibrary.com/cdsr/doi/10.1002/14651858.CD006850.pub3/pdf/CDSR/CD006850/CD006850_abstract.pdf)). For neurologically intact thoracolumbar burst fractures, "The decision to use an external brace is at the discretion of the treating physician", because outcomes are equivalent with or without a brace (Grade B, though the abstract's "is an option" wording corresponds to Grade C in the guideline's own nomenclature) ([CNS 2018](https://www.cns.org/guidelines/browse-guidelines-detail/8-nonoperative-care)). For positional plagiocephaly, helmet recommendations carry "uncertain clinical certainty", while the one RCT (84 infants) "found no added value" (26% vs 23% full normalisation, with only 21% of eligible infants recruited) ([CNS 2016](https://www.cns.org/guidelines/management-positional-plagiocephaly/5-role-of-cranial-molding-orthosis-helmet-therapy)). [UNKNOWN] Not found or not readable: an AACPDM orthosis pathway, a Ponseti clubfoot guideline, a post-polio guideline, the 2018 Duchenne care statements, SRS bracing criteria, the AFO wording of NICE NG236 and AHA/ASA 2016, and the status of Cochrane review CD011249.

[DIRECT] ISPO's 2009 cerebral palsy consensus describes the evidence as remaining "at a weak level or ... inconclusive": AFOs "that prevent plantarflexion can improve gait efficiency", but there is "inconclusive evidence of the effect of AFOs on children's motor function", "no evidence that hip orthoses (for example the SWASH orthosis) prevent progressive hip subluxation/dislocation", spinal orthoses "do not appear to prevent progression of scoliosis in children with cerebral palsy", and there is "no good evidence" that wrist–hand orthoses prevent deformity ([ISPO 2009, third-party copy](https://braceworks.ca/wp-content/uploads/2016/08/ispo-Recent-Developments-in-Healthcare-for-Cerebral-Palsy-Implications-and-Opportunities-for-Orthotics-oxford-2008.pdf)); the journal paper from the same meeting reports "substantial evidence" for gait efficiency at GMFCS I–III ([Morris et al. 2011](https://strathprints.strath.ac.uk/40443/)). [INFERRED] The difference is the outcome (laboratory gait efficiency against motor function and daily mobility), so items must be tagged by outcome.

[DIRECT] Reviews from 2018–2026 mostly report low-certainty, short-term or laboratory outcomes. In cerebral palsy, AFOs gave a small gait-speed gain (SMD 0.28 in 884 children, called "strong" by the authors' own grading) ([Lintanf et al. 2018](https://search.pedro.org.au/search-results/record-detail/54734)), improved dorsiflexion at initial contact by 9.66°, with flexible AFOs preserving push-off power better than rigid ones, in studies of low quality ([Faccioli et al. 2025, CC BY](https://www.mdpi.com/2673-1592/7/1/13)), while comparisons between AFO types remain inconclusive ([Aboutorabi et al. 2017](https://www.em-consulte.com/article/1170105/resume/efficacy-of-ankle-foot-orthoses-types-on-walking-i)); serial casting is rated effective for early contracture, but the AFO row's rating was lost in extraction ([Novak et al. 2020, CC BY](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s11910-020-1022-z.pdf)). After stroke, AFOs immediately improve walking energy metrics on "generally weak" evidence, with no difference between types in three small comparisons ([Daryabor et al. 2020](https://ira.lib.polyu.edu.hk/bitstream/10397/92884/1/Daryabor_Effect_Types_Ankle-foot.pdf)). A tuning study of 18 children found "Little evidence ... for an optimal SVA of 10–12° at midstance" ([Oudenhoven et al. 2022](https://documentserver.uhasselt.be/bitstream/1942/36178/3/How%20does%20a%20systematic%20tuning%20protocol%20for%20ankle%20foot%20orthosis%20footwear%20combinations%20affect%20gait%20in%20children%20in%20cerebral%20palsy.pdf)); 3D-printed AFOs perform like conventional ones in immediate-effect studies, called "emerging evidence" ([Pollen et al. 2025](https://scholars.hkbu.edu.hk/en/publications/effects-of-3d-printed-ankle-foot-orthoses-on-gait-a-systematic-re/)). For plantar heel pain, one review found a medium-term benefit over sham (SMD −0.27, moderate certainty) that is below the minimal important difference ([Whittaker et al. 2018](https://bjsm.bmj.com/content/52/5/322)) and another found orthoses "not superior ... compared with sham" ([BJSM 52(16)](https://bjsm.bmj.com/content/52/16/1040)); both found custom no better than prefabricated. Night-only scoliosis bracing has "no evidence" in a 2024 review ([Colak et al.](https://openaccess.bilgi.edu.tr/items/eefc84e7-3597-4b87-8996-e15091f0a166/full)) while the BASIS non-inferiority trial (planned n = 780) has no results yet ([BASIS protocol](https://boneandjoint.org.uk/Article/10.1302/2633-1462.411.BJO-2023-0128)). Splinting and stretching gave no clinically important gain in joint mobility at GRADE high certainty ([Cochrane CD007455](https://www.cochranelibrary.com/cdsr/doi/10.1002/14651858.CD007455.pub3/pdf/CDSR/CD007455/CD007455_abstract.pdf)), and an RCT concluded "routine wrist splinting soon after stroke should be discontinued" ([Lannin et al. 2007](https://ro.uow.edu.au/hbspapers/845)). For knee instability, KAFO and stance-control KAFO evidence is weak (21 studies, 2 RCTs) ([McDaid et al. 2017, CC BY](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/121684/1/e015927.full.pdf)), and no review after 2017 was found. [INFERRED] Orthotic conflicts are mostly about certainty, comparator or outcome rather than opposite directions, and no verified source supports choosing one stroke AFO type over another, so the system reports effects, populations and limitations, never a device type.

[DIRECT] No validated, general-purpose orthotic assessment form was found; ICRC's AFO and KAFO manuals say assessment follows "international P&O standards" without listing them ([ICRC AFO manual](https://www.icrc.org/en/doc/assets/files/other/eng-afo-2010.pdf)). [PROPOSED] A minimum data set can still be assembled from items the verified sources treat as decision-relevant: a core block in which diagnosis or aetiology with side and level, age and skeletal maturity, time since onset, primary goals, and protective sensation and skin are critical, and weight, footwear (including heel pitch), cognition and donning ability, and device history with adherence are important; plus condition modules (for stroke, ambulatory status and a named gait-speed test, plantarflexion range with the knee extended and flexed, tone with scale, grade, muscle and position, and pacemaker or epilepsy if FES is discussed; for cerebral palsy, GMFCS level, dorsiflexion range, recent botulinum toxin, casting or surgery, and gait pattern; for scoliosis, Risser stage, Cobb angle with radiograph date, apex and previous bracing; for the diabetic foot, loss of protective sensation, peripheral artery disease, ulcer and amputation history, ulcer site, and infection and ischaemia grades; for spinal fracture, neurological status, level, kyphosis and the surgeon's plan). If any critical field is missing, the system returns only "information needed" and no pathway. The KAFO module and "sex" for scoliosis are inferences that an orthotist and a rehabilitation physician must confirm. [DIRECT] A tone value means little without its scale: in spinal cord injury, Modified Ashworth reliability ranged κ 0.531–0.774, while one Modified Tardieu measure had poor inter-rater reliability (hip adductors ICC 0.248) ([Akpinar et al. 2017](https://www.nature.com/articles/sc201748)); GMFCS materials are McMaster copyright, need a licence for health-record integration, and no Arabic GMFCS-E&R was listed ([CanChild](https://canchild.ca/get-involved/use-of-materials-translation-guidelines/)).

[DIRECT] Design rules are mostly technical guidance: ICRC's AFO manual specifies trim lines (2 cm behind the malleolar tips for a flexible AFO, 1 cm in front for a rigid one), 5° dorsiflexion preload, polypropylene of 3, 4 or 5 mm by body weight and EVA lining when sensation is impaired, citing no outcome studies ([ICRC AFO](https://www.icrc.org/en/doc/assets/files/other/eng-afo-2010.pdf)); the KAFO manual is "© ICRC, September 2006" with no reuse statement ([ICRC KAFO](https://www.icrc.org/en/doc/assets/files/other/eng-kafo.pdf)). [PROPOSED] Treat ICRC design content as Layer 1 "technical, no outcome evidence", valuable for low-resource settings and teaching but never quoted as proof that a design works. [DIRECT] Among outcome measures, OPUS has been validated in several languages (reliability 0.62–0.95) with no Arabic version confirmed ([OPUS review](https://zaguan.unizar.es/record/149109?ln=en)), an Arabic QUEST 2.0 was validated in 100 orthosis users (device subscale α 0.83, services 0.89) ([A-QUEST 2.0](https://uninsubria.unifind.cineca.it/resource/item/122337)), and SRS-22r is "free for anyone to use" with an Arabic version listed ([SRS](https://srs.org/Research/Patient-Outcome-Questionnaires)); these are the only verified Arabic, orthosis-relevant patient-reported measures.

[DIRECT] Most key orthotic guidelines (ANPT, IWGDF, NICE, CNS) and the ICRC manuals are rights-reserved or carry no licence, while SOSORT, Novak, Faccioli and the osteoporotic-fracture mini review are CC BY ([Borg et al. 2024](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/rehabilitation-sciences/articles/10.3389/fresc.2024.1506279/full)) and the tuning study and BASIS are CC BY-NC-ND. [PROPOSED] The orthotics research suggests a "summary only" statement type (a reviewer's paraphrase with a dated badge, excluded from the word-for-word check) until permission arrives. [INFERRED] That departs from the owner's principle that every sentence is backed by the source's own words, so it needs the owner's explicit decision; the alternative that keeps the principle intact is written permission requests to APTA/ANPT, IWGDF, ICRC and ISPO, with those guidelines held as metadata until then. [PROPOSED] Five fake golden cases start the orthotics benchmark: GC-ORT-01, chronic stroke with foot drop and a request to "reduce calf tightness" (must flag plantarflexion range with the knee extended and a tone scale; may show ANPT gait-speed and "should not provide for spasticity" statements; must never choose AFO or FES; run in English and Arabic); GC-ORT-02, a child with cerebral palsy at GMFCS II whose parent wants "the best AFO" tuned to 10–12° (must flag dorsiflexion range; may not support a best type, deformity prevention or a fixed angle); GC-ORT-03, a neuropathic plantar ulcer with infection and arterial status missing (pathway blocked); GC-ORT-04, a 13-year-old with adolescent idiopathic scoliosis (Risser 1, Cobb 28°) whose family wants night-only bracing (equivalence not supported; never prescribe hours); and GC-ORT-05, a neurologically intact L1 burst fracture asking whether a TLSO is mandatory (Grade B physician discretion; route to the surgeon).

### Gait and biomechanics: validated in healthy adults, untested on prosthetic limbs

[DIRECT] The reference knowledge consists of conventions and vocabularies: the ISB 2002 joint coordinate system recommendation ([Crossref](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1016/S0021-9290(01)00222-6)), ISO 29783 vocabularies for normal (2008), prosthetic (2015) and pathological gait (2016), usable only as metadata ([ISO/TC 168](https://www.iso.org/committee/53630/x/catalogue/p/1/u/0/w/0/d/0)), the 1992 Atlas gait chapters (expert, reproduced with AAOS permission) ([Atlas](http://www.oandplibrary.org/alp/)), and the VA/DoD gait tool described in §6. None of the verified sources tests which cause produces a given prosthetic gait deviation. [INFERRED] Deviation-to-cause tables are Layer 1 expert knowledge that may flag an observation for specialist assessment but never attribute a cause.

[DIRECT] Gait technology is validated mainly in healthy adults. OpenCap's multi-camera system had a joint-angle error of 4.5° (range 1.7–10.3°) and ground-reaction-force error of 6.2% body weight in 10 healthy adults, and its authors say validation in target populations is still needed and that its marker model "may not generalize" ([OpenCap](https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371/journal.pcbi.1011462)); a single-phone version reports 4.8° without stating the population in its abstract ([OpenCap Monocular](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.24733)). In 30 children with cerebral palsy, markerless capture tracked sagittal knee and ankle angles well but missed the pelvic tilt and transverse hip deviations typical of the condition ([Wishaupt et al. 2024](https://doaj.org/article/795d462de25845db89d461bee75a69d9)); in 30 transtibial prosthesis users, IMUs agreed with optical capture in the sagittal plane but poorly in frontal and transverse planes, and ankle agreement varied with prosthetic foot type ([Sensors 2023](https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/23/3/1738/htm)); and a lower-back IMU pipeline validated in six clinical cohorts without amputees had cadence errors of 6.6–11.8% and stride-length errors of 0.15–0.33 m, worse below 0.5 m/s ([Mobilise-D](https://jneuroengrehab.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s12984-023-01198-5)). No validation of markerless video in lower-limb prosthesis users was found. OpenPose and AlphaPose are non-commercial, the SMPL body model required by WHAM forbids commercial use ([SMPL licence](https://smpl.is.tue.mpg.de/modellicense.html)), Ultralytics and the gaitmap_mad package are AGPL-3.0, MediaPipe, MMPose and rtmlib are Apache-2.0, the ezc3d reader is MIT ([ezc3d](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/pyomeca/ezc3d/dev/LICENSE)), and AddBiomechanics automatically shares every upload with the community under CC BY 4.0 ([AddBiomechanics](https://addbiomechanics.org/)). The FDA treats software that analyses a medical image or a pattern of repeated measurements from a signal-acquisition system, including wearables, as a device function ([FDA CDS guidance](https://www.fda.gov/media/109618/download)).

[PROPOSED] The gait module should start as a results importer: C3D or CSV files read with ezc3d plus a manual form for lab-report values (speed, cadence, step-length asymmetry, joint-angle peaks), deterministic indicator rules whose thresholds come from cited sources and whose wording is "needs specialist assessment", and the existing retrieval restating what sources say about a flagged pattern. A local video timing tool (cadence, step and stance time, symmetry, built on Apache or BSD libraries with a "research-grade, unvalidated in prosthesis users" badge) and IMU walking-bout detection (mobgap, Apache-2.0, whose maintainers urge validation outside their cohorts ([mobgap](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mobilise-d/mobgap/main/README.md))) belong to research use only, because signal analysis moves the tool into device territory (§14). Safe to show, each with its caveat: walking speed from a timed distance, cadence, step-time symmetry, and sagittal knee and ankle trends on the intact limb. Not to show: joint moments and powers from video, pelvic tilt, transverse rotations and frontal ankle or knee angles, any prosthesis-side video kinematics, and K-levels inferred from gait. Video is processed locally, raw footage is discarded after keypoints by default, faces are blurred if footage must be kept, and nothing is uploaded to auto-sharing services; GDPR, used here only as a reference framework, treats health and biometric data as special categories ([GDPR Art. 9](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-9-gdpr/)).

### Rehabilitation and follow-up: doses and intervals are not established

[DIRECT] Beyond VA/DoD, two guideline sources add most: the Dutch VRA guideline (2020; 16 GRADE modules, available in Dutch only, with no follow-up module) ([Richtlijnendatabase](https://richtlijnendatabase.nl/richtlijn/amputatie_prothesiologie_onderste_extremiteit)) and BACPAR's pre- and post-operative physiotherapy guideline (2nd edition, 2016, grades A–D, many at C, meaning consensus) ([BACPAR 2016](https://vascularsociety.org.uk/_userfiles/pages/files/guidelines/preandpostopguidelines-recommendations.pdf)). Multicomponent exercise and gait training helps prosthesis users (SMD 0.42 subacute, low to moderate certainty; 0.67 chronic, moderate), but the doses studied ranged from 30 minutes once to seven times a week for 2–12 weeks (subacute) to 15–60 minutes two or three times a week for 4–16 weeks (chronic), and the optimum is "unclear" ([Madou et al. 2024](https://search.pedro.org.au/search-results/record-detail/78969)); a 2026 meta-analysis found **only 7 of 54 outcomes reached minimal clinically important or detectable change** ([Dhariwal et al. 2026](https://research.monash.edu/en/publications/multilevel-meta-analysis-of-therapeutic-interventions-to-improve-/)). The Dutch guideline recommends offering walking-skill training when a first or follow-up prosthesis is provided and agreeing intensity and duration with the patient ([Dutch gait module](https://richtlijnendatabase.nl/richtlijn/amputatie_prothesiologie_onderste_extremiteit/looptraining_na_amputatie.html)). For falls, BACPAR grades falls education B, but "no evidence-based protocol has been proven to reduce falls in amputees", and published TUG fall cut-offs conflict (over 19 s against 8.17 or 9.25 s) ([Clemens et al. 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8594758)). For phantom limb pain, the Dutch guideline says "consider gabapentin" although its low-certainty evidence finds gabapentin "similar to placebo", and "consider mirror therapy early" although low-certainty evidence shows no difference against an active comparator ([Dutch phantom-pain module](https://richtlijnendatabase.nl/richtlijn/amputatie_prothesiologie_onderste_extremiteit/behandeling_fantoompijn_na_amputatie.html)), while VA/DoD is weak for mirror therapy and neither for drugs, and Cochrane finds drug evidence inconclusive ([Cochrane CD006380](https://cochranelibrary.com/cdsr/doi/10.1002/14651858.CD006380.pub3/pdf/CDSR/CD006380/CD006380_abstract.pdf)). Upper-limb training shows a "lack of evidence regarding the most effective training techniques" ([Parr et al. 2022](https://e-space.mmu.ac.uk/628913/3/A%20sco**%20review%20of%20the%20application%20of%20motor%20learning%20principles%20to%20optimise%20myoelectric%20prosthetic%20hand%20control.pdf)). [INFERRED] The system should show the doses studied and never present a dose as evidence-based, and should judge change against source-matched thresholds, not p-values.

[DIRECT] How devices and training interact is barely studied. Giving an AFO in week 1 rather than week 9 after stroke produced earlier gains but no difference at 26 weeks, and the early group fell more in weeks 1–8, mostly while not wearing the AFO, among people with Functional Ambulation Category 3 or less and Berg scores below 45 ([EVOLUTIONS thesis summary](https://www.revalidatie.nl/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/corien_nikamp-simons_-_summary.pdf)); 26 weeks of AFO use did not weaken tibialis anterior activity ([Nikamp et al.](https://www.medicaljournals.se/jrm/content/abstract/10.2340/16501977-2498)); FES and AFO gave comparable walking gains across five RCTs of 815 people ([Prenton et al. 2016](https://pure.hud.ac.uk/en/publications/functional-electrical-stimulation-versus-ankle-foot-orthoses-for-/)); evidence for combining AFOs or KAFOs with therapy in cerebral palsy is not sufficient (11 RCTs, 10 at high risk of bias) ([Miccinilli et al. 2024](https://doaj.org/article/accf2daeaa214169a7113e915df64d87)); supervised Schroth exercise added to bracing reduced the Cobb angle by 3.65° more than bracing alone in 80 adolescents ([Kyrkousis et al. 2024](https://doaj.org/article/7bad1f0059694d959ac3b4aeeec1fd73)); and 54.3% of stroke patients given an AFO had stopped using it ([Safaz et al. 2015](https://avesis.bozok.edu.tr/yayin/8575afe1-15f2-4dff-81f7-8a48a13c8167/use-and-abandonment-rates-of-assistive-devices-orthoses-in-patients-with-stroke)). [INFERRED] For orthosis-plus-therapy in cerebral palsy, vertebral fractures and the upper limb, the answer is "No sufficient evidence identified"; the early-AFO falls finding is a concrete, single-trial safety-education trigger; and "device in use?" should be a mandatory field at every orthosis follow-up.

[DIRECT] No sufficient evidence identified for evidence-based follow-up or device-replacement intervals in adults. The WHO P&O Standards say "The need of many users is lifelong, and their devices must be replaced regularly as they wear out or no longer fit" and define indicators for follow-up, repair, replacement and abandonment but no frequency ([WHO P&O Standards, Part 2](https://medbox.org/index.php/dl/5e148832db60a2044c2d50ab)); a cerebral palsy programme reviews children "every 6 months until the age of 6 years, and then once a year until they are 16" ([CPIP](https://www.sfh-tr.nhs.uk/services/paediatric-musculoskeletal/cerebral-palsy-integrated-pathway-cpip-hip-surveillance)); the Swedish amputation registry measures outcomes at baseline, 12 and 24 months and reports a median of 69 days (transtibial) and 97 days (transfemoral) to the first individual prosthesis ([SwedeAmp](https://actaorthop.org/actao/article/view/625)); residual-limb volume fell 17–35% over 160 days in one small vascular cohort, mostly stabilising by about 120 days, donning became difficult when volume rose 3–5%, and one 5-ply sock equals about 5.2% of limb volume ([Sanders & Fatone](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4423813/)); and in a 1998 population study 26% of exchange sockets were delivered in the first year ([Hermodsson & Persson](https://actaorthop.org/actao/article/view/20650)). Abandonment is substantial: 11–22% of lower-limb users at about one year (§6), 54% of AFO users in one cohort, and 30–80% of upper-limb users in reviews ([Collu et al. 2025](https://www.mdpi.com/2673-1592/7/6/167)).

[DIRECT] No single core outcome set exists: ISPO COMPASS uses AMP, TUG and the two-minute walk plus PEQ subscales and TAPES-R ([COMPASS](https://atscalepartnership.org/s/ispo_compass_user_guide_2021.pdf)), the AAOP toolkit uses EQ-5D-5L, PLUS-M, Socket Comfort Score, 10-metre walk, TUG and AMP ([AAOP](https://www.oandp.org/-lower-limb-prosthetics-outcome-measures-toolkit-summary)), the Dutch set uses the L-test, 6MWT, AMPPRO and SIGAM/WAP ([Dutch clinimetrics](https://richtlijnendatabase.nl/richtlijn/amputatie_prothesiologie_onderste_extremiteit/klinimetrie_na_amputatie.html)), and SwedeAmp uses EQ-5D-5L, LCI-5 and a Prosthetic Use Score. [PROPOSED] Flag a change as "exceeds measurement error" only against an MDC for the same instrument, version and population, call it "clinically important" only when a matching MCID exists (otherwise "MCID not established"), never judge change from a predicted 6MWT (whose prediction error, 29.6 m, approaches the 45 m MDC), keep an instrument registry tagged by which panel recommends each measure, and never let the system choose the core set.

[PROPOSED] For use case 07, store FHIR-shaped SQLite tables (episode, encounter with trigger codes, an instrument registry holding MDCs, MCIDs and licence terms, observations with test conditions, findings, devices whose `prescribed_by` is always the specialist, device events linked to "training offered", device usage with non-use reasons, goals, plan activities whose `dose_basis` is "local protocol", "studied in [source]" or "not established", and a computed change flag that is never stored as a conclusion), adopt FHIR element names, UCUM units and LOINC or "X" codes internally, use ICF codes only as optional tags behind a licence check (never WHO descriptions or Arabic machine translations), export FHIR later, build no FHIR server, and never claim an "ICF Core Set for amputation". ### Education: LLM drafts need specialist review before any learner sees them

[DIRECT] The largest review of LLM-generated multiple-choice questions (71 studies) found error rates from under 1% to 45% and concluded that "LLMs appear useful drafting tools but current evidence does not yet support unsupervised use in summative assessment" ([Kıyak et al. 2026](https://tipegitimi.gazi.edu.tr/ai-meded-observatory/articles/validity-of-ai-generated-multiple-choice-questions-in-medical-education-a-systematic-review)); retrieval-built case questions showed inter-rater agreement near zero (α ≈ 0.05) with inflated LLM-judge scores and no non-RAG baseline ([MedTutor](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.06979v2)); grounding a model in notes raised answer consistency (κ 0.927 against 0.679) but not accuracy (74.4% against 84.3% for an ungrounded model) ([Lin & Cho 2026](https://formative.jmir.org/2026/1/e88008/PDF)); and in a simulation pilot, teams "often deferred decisions to the tool" and errors appeared when prompting in Finnish ([Gustafsson et al. 2026](https://mededu.jmir.org/2026/1/e79134)). [UNKNOWN] No controlled comparison of grounded and ungrounded question generation, and no AI-generated questions in P&O education, were found. [PROPOSED] Use case 06 reuses the same pipeline: every factual sentence in a key rationale or explanation is quote-checked, each distractor needs a quote showing it is wrong for this case or a reviewer's confirmation, single-best-answer keys come only from Layers 1–2, Layer 3 appears only in "how certain is the evidence?" items, Layer 4 is never used, conflicting topics become "compare the sources" items, and "neither" topics are taught as "no sufficient evidence". [INFERRED] The earlier MCQ app can supply the quiz flow, item storage and extractive question answering, but its generic WordNet distractors are unlikely to suit clinical content.

[DIRECT] The prototype's Arabic interface text was written by Claude and has not been checked by an Arabic-reading prosthetist; its 225-row Arabic term sheet is the project's only glossary, and Arabic sources are never cited ([handoff](/root/.claude/uploads/830110ea-030d-5d0c-9ab6-615062c1589f/85794c40-handoff_prompt_2026-10-11.md)). No Arabic ICRC P&O manual and no public Arabic P&O glossary from any degree programme were found. [INFERRED] No shippable Arabic P&O lexicon exists, so the project's sheet must remain the master, each Arabic term carrying a pointer to where it was observed (UMD lookup and date, WHO Arabic Standards page, EDA guide, GSO or SFDA title, reviewer) rather than copied text, with a licence column per row because share-alike and non-commercial rows cannot be merged without carrying both conditions.

[DIRECT] No study of P&O terminology as practised in Syria or the Levant was found, and official bodies already disagree on the field's core terms: SFDA writes "الأطراف الصناعية وتقويم العظام" and uses "الأجهزة التعويضية" for external orthoses ([SFDA](https://sfda.gov.sa/en/node/11130)), GSO writes "الأطراف الصناعية والتركيبات التقويمية", the Egyptian guide "الأطراف الصناعية والأجهزة التقويمية", and one Syrian Arab Red Crescent page uses both "الأطراف الصناعية" and "الأطراف الاصطناعية" ([SARC](https://sarc.sy/?p=19601)). Arabic medical terminology has been described as "in a state of chaos due to the plurality of Arabic terms for the same scientific concept" ([Kassem Sara 2019](https://benjamins.com/online/hot/articles/med1)), Jordanian doctors code-switch to bridge lexical gaps ([Alkhlaifat et al. 2020](https://researchers.westernsydney.edu.au/en/publications/code-switching-between-arabic-and-english-during-jordanian-gp-con/)), Syria was the only country with an Arabic medical curriculum among five surveyed (1,727 students), where 73.7% of students prefer it ([Darwish et al. 2026](https://www.dovepress.com/arab-medical-students-attitudes-toward-the-arabization-of-medical-educ-peer-reviewed-fulltext-article-AMEP)), and frontier LLMs left English terms untranslated in 20.2% ± 12.8% of Arabic output ([Anyaegbuna et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.22642)). [INFERRED] Four registers will coexist (formal standard Arabic, regulatory variants, practitioner usage with loanwords and descriptive forms such as "فوق الركبة", and lay terms), so the glossary needs a register field, and an output convention of "Arabic preferred term (English canonical term)" at first mention suits readers trained in Arabic who also read English sources. [PROPOSED] Collect variants empirically by asking two or three Syrian or Levantine prosthetists and physiotherapists to write how they would ask about 30 common situations, and mine their forms into admitted or variant rows tagged by country.

[DIRECT] For matching, Lucene's Arabic normaliser folds hamza-on-alef, teh marbuta, alef maksura, diacritics and tatweel ([Lucene](https://lucene.apache.org/core/9_12_0/analysis/common/org/apache/lucene/analysis/ar/ArabicNormalizer.html)); CAMeL Tools is MIT-licensed, supports Python 3.11–3.14 and was tested on Windows 10, though its dialect identification is unavailable there ([CAMeL Tools](https://github.com/CAMeL-Lab/camel_tools)); Farasa is "strictly" for research use and needs Java ([farasapy](https://pypi.org/project/farasapy)). [PROPOSED] Use one normaliser for keys and checks only, never for display, applying in order NFKC, removal of tatweel and diacritics, أ/إ/آ/ٱ → ا, ى → ي, ة → ه, Arabic-Indic and extended digits → ASCII, Persian look-alikes ی and ک → ي and ك, and whitespace collapse; store broken plurals such as طرف/أطراف and جهاز/أجهزة as explicit variants; strip leading clitics (و، ف، ب، ل، ك and ال) with a minimum stem length; and measure 30 Syrian-written queries raw, normalised and with glossary expansion.

[DIRECT] Evidence on Arabic medical translation is thin and metric-based. Google Translate beat GPT-4o on surface metrics for an Arabic clinical summary (chrF 0.5907 vs 0.4957) with no human rating ([Li et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.16601v1)); four frontier LLMs reached Arabic back-translation similarity of 0.976 but the study did no error analysis, and its authors warn that such metrics "may miss clinically critical errors such as hallucinated dosages, reversed polarities" ([Anyaegbuna et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.22642)); and a widely cited Google Translate study did not evaluate Arabic at all ([Taira et al. 2021](https://synapse.mskcc.org/synapse/works/190552)). The strongest terminology evidence is from other languages: with a correct glossary, top systems reached 98–99% term accuracy, but random glossaries cut one strong system to 49.2% ([WMT25 terminology](https://aclanthology.org/2025.wmt-1.30.pdf)); retrieved demonstrations "consistently outperform terminology" ([Li, Luo, Briakou & Cherry 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.05010)); and reference-based metrics beat LLM judges at segment level, with robustness across languages "a major challenge" ([WMT25 metrics](https://www.dfki.de/web/forschung/projekte-publikationen/publikation/16514)). [UNKNOWN] No study measured Arabic negation, laterality, number or abbreviation errors in medical translation, and no Arabic glossary-constrained medical evaluation exists. [INFERRED] Laterality is a grammar trap, because the side adjective agrees with the noun's gender (الساق اليمنى/اليسرى but الطرف الأيمن/الأيسر); glossary quality matters more than the choice of translation model, and unreviewed rows can make results worse than no glossary.

[DIRECT] With few experts, error-span annotation (spans marked minor or major plus a 0–100 score) was 32% faster per annotator than full MQM, ranked systems as accurately, and had higher inter-annotator agreement (Kendall τ 0.254 vs 0.116) ([Kocmi et al. 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.wmt-1.131.pdf)); reviews warn of "a tendency to overtrust LLM judgments in multilingual settings" ([Doğruöz et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02235)); and BERTScore "failed to fully measure appropriateness or trustworthiness" in Arabic health QA, separating the best and worst teams by 0.011 ([AraHealthQA 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.20047v2)). [PROPOSED] Each Arabic sentence is reviewed beside its English quote and English sentence: the reviewer marks error spans as minor, major or critical (critical meaning it could change a decision: side, level, number, negation, modality or device class), tags each with an MQM-Core category, gives a 0–100 score, answers "safe to show to a specialist: yes/no", and may propose a correction that becomes a reference and an approved example. [INFERRED] Zero critical errors in 60 reviewed sentences bounds the rate below about 5%, and in 100 below about 3%; estimating a proportion to ±10 points needs about 96 items, sampled with sentences containing laterality, level, number or negation tokens first. [PROPOSED] About 25 sentences per release are double-rated, with Cohen's κ reported on "safe" and "any major or critical error"; back-translation is only an automatic screen; any LLM judge is one of two different models used as flaggers after calibration on 100 or more human labels, never as an approver; and chrF and COMET-22 (Apache-2.0, unlike the non-commercial CometKiwi) serve only as regression tripwires ([COMET-22](https://huggingface.co/Unbabel/wmt22-comet-da)).

[DIRECT] The W3C's rules for right-to-left pages are simple: set `dir="rtl"` and `lang` on the `html` element rather than in CSS; "tightly wrap every opposite-direction phrase", using `<bdi>` or `dir="auto"` when direction is unknown; put `dir="auto"` and `dirname` on inputs; and prefer markup to control characters, since the problem "is worse in applications that drop text into a page, say from a database" ([W3C inline bidi](https://www.w3.org/International/articles/inline-bidi-markup/); [W3C structural markup](https://www.w3.org/International/questions/qa-html-dir)). [UNKNOWN] Syrian clinical digit conventions and the usability of bilingual decision-support layouts are unstudied.

### A glossary-driven rendering step with nine deterministic checks

[DIRECT] TBX-Basic, compliant with ISO 30042, models terms with a usage status of preferred, admitted or deprecated ("Only one value is permitted for each term"), ISO 3166 country codes for geographical usage, grammatical gender and term type ([TBX-Basic v4](https://terminorgs.net/downloads/TBX-Basic-V4.pdf)). [PROPOSED] The glossary becomes two tables. A concept table holds one row per English concept (a stable id such as `PO-TT-001`, domain, preferred and admitted English terms including abbreviations, a definition in the team's own words with its source cited, ISO only by clause number, a risk class of critical, high or normal, ICD-11 and ICF mapping fields, the row's licence and status). A term table holds one row per Arabic surface form (the term as written, its normalised key, usage status with exactly one preferred term per concept and register, term type including transliteration and descriptive forms, register, country, grammatical gender, plural forms, the source where it was observed, an example sentence, and review status, reviewer role, date and version). The Arabic step then detects glossary concepts in the verified English sentence, replaces numbers, ranges, units and codes (K0–K4, L-codes, ICD codes) with placeholders, prompts with the English quote, the English sentence, a must-use table of preferred Arabic terms (with deprecated forms listed as forbidden) and two or three approved example pairs, restores placeholders verbatim, and adds the English canonical term in parentheses after the first Arabic mention of each critical concept. Nine deterministic checks follow: glossary adherence (preferred or admitted form present, deprecated form absent, matched on normalised keys with clitic and plural tolerance); the same multiset of numbers as the English sentence after digit normalisation, with units unchanged; byte-identical codes; the same set of sides, accepting both gender forms; identical amputation-level and K-level concept ids; parity of negation cues (لا، لم، لن، ليس، غير، دون، بدون، عدم), which flags rather than fails because Arabic can negate lexically; the same modality class (يجب/ينبغي against قد/يمكن), also a flag; no stray Latin tokens outside codes, units and parenthesised glossary terms; and an Arabic-to-English length ratio within the band seen in approved examples.

[PROPOSED] Each release reports, on a frozen set, glossary adherence (target at least 99% on critical concepts, mirroring the sentence-level WMT25 results), critical-token fidelity (target 100%, any failure blocking), the share of sentences with a major or critical error with its rule-of-three bound, the "safe to show" rate, κ on double-rated items, chrF and COMET-22 tripwires, Arabic–English parity of kept items when the same case is entered in each language, and reviewer minutes per item. The minimum expert effort is a one-off Tier 0 of about 4–8 hours, in which an Arabic-reading prosthetist reviews the 225 rows critical class first and a second Levantine reviewer settles disputes (about 1–2 minutes per row, to be measured); a Tier 1 of about 2 hours per release for 100 stratified sentences (at least half containing critical tokens), about 25 of them double-rated, plus every flagged item; and a Tier 2 cache that reuses approved renderings by a hash of quote, English sentence and glossary version, so that only new or changed items reach reviewers. [UNKNOWN] Real annotation speed and agreement for bilingual P&O clinicians are planning assumptions until a pilot measures them. Before any Arabic source is ever cited, the quote checker needs the normaliser for keys only, Arabic sources need their own evidence-layer tags, retrieval needs per-language quotas because English queries over Arabic documents drop sharply (§2), and each source's licence must be checked; today only WHO's Arabic Standards and CC0 or share-alike wiki data are clear.


## 9. Licences decide what can be quoted word for word

[DIRECT] The quote-checked design needs stored full text, and several of the most authoritative sources forbid exactly that. ISO's terms require a separate licence for any AI or machine-learning use, including feeding content into AI systems and prompting AI tools, opt out of the EU text-and-data-mining exception, and prohibit systematic extraction and indexing ([ISO terms](https://www.iso.org/home/store/iso-obp-terms.html)). Cochrane reserves all rights "including text and data mining and AI training" and requires permission for machine access ([Cochrane](https://www.cochranelibrary.com/about/open-access)). ## 10. Four evidence layers, native grades and a small hand-built graph

[DIRECT] The brief requires four evidence layers that must never be merged, and the project rule is that clinical evidence values come only from the owner ([handoff](/root/.claude/uploads/830110ea-030d-5d0c-9ab6-615062c1589f/85794c40-handoff_prompt_2026-10-11.md)). [UNKNOWN] No published evidence-layer taxonomy for P&O was found, so the definitions below are the owner's scheme as applied by the researchers. [PROPOSED] Assign the layer per knowledge item rather than per source, store each source's native grade unchanged next to it (GRADE strength and certainty, SIGN A–D, SoRT A–C, AHRQ strength of evidence, NICE procedure categories, ANPT levels I–V with grades A–D/P/R, CNS classes and levels, Delphi consensus, or "ungraded"), keep certainty as a separate field, and never convert one scale into another.

| Layer | What belongs in it | Display rule |
|---|---|---|
| L1 Established knowledge | Definitions, classifications, terminology, biomechanics conventions, service standards, descriptive textbook and technical content; no effectiveness claims | Background, labelled "descriptive / expert" |
| L2 Evidence-based practice | Statements produced by an explicit appraisal method: guideline recommendations of any strength including "neither", review conclusions with their certainty, procedure guidance, formal consensus (flagged) | Always shown with native grade, population and year |
| L3 Emerging research | Single primary studies, studies published after the relevant guideline's evidence cut-off, low-certainty reviews of new technology, "preliminary" findings, dataset reference values | A separate "emerging evidence" section, never as recommendations |
| L4 Experimental or hypothesis | Bench and laboratory results, case reports, abstracts, preprints, manufacturer claims, model-generated inferences | Never presented as clinical fact; hidden by default or in a labelled research panel |

[PROPOSED] Flags travel with items: `possibly_superseded` (older than about ten years, or a newer edition exists), `industry_affiliated`, `payer_policy` (CMS content, kept outside the layers), `paediatric`, `jurisdiction`, `nc_licence`, `nd_licence`, `consensus_only`, `expert_descriptive`, `no_outcome_evidence`, `emerging_technology`, `pending_trial`, `internal_inconsistency` and `research_methods_only` (the 2026 SRS–SOSORT research consensus, which must never answer clinical questions). Evidence cut-offs decide when a study is "emerging": 15 March 2024 for VA/DoD LLA, March 2019 for the Dutch guideline, 2 May 2017 for the Hanger transtibial guideline, December 2018 for the Cochrane dressing review, November 2019 for ANPT and 31 March 2015 for CNS 2018. ### A hand-curated schema graph, not an LLM-built GraphRAG index

[DIRECT] GraphRAG's gains concentrate in multi-hop and summarisation questions, its token costs run to 40–350 times plain RAG for some variants, some variants handle no-answer questions worse ([GraphRAG-Bench](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.05690v3); [Han et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.11371v2)), and an LLM-built triplet graph contained only about 65.8% of answer entities on one benchmark ([Han et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.11371v2)). [PROPOSED] The brief's knowledge graph should be a small, hand-curated, versioned schema graph (YAML, roughly 50–200 typed nodes and edges, owned by the owner and a specialist) that follows the brief's chain of patient characteristics → functional status → amputation level → residual-limb characteristics → biomechanics → gait parameters → clinical goals → components → socket → alignment → rehabilitation → functional outcomes. It decides which intake fields matter for which topics (use case 01), supplies topic templates per domain, enforces applicability gates such as "functional level only for lower limb", and links populations so the evidence-population mismatch warning can fire. It needs no graph database and no LLM extraction. A GraphRAG pilot is justified only if the miss analysis shows genuinely multi-hop failures (for example gait deviation → alignment parameter → component) that query decomposition cannot fix, and only with a variant costing about 1,000 tokens per query.

[PROPOSED] Each domain becomes a versioned pack that one engine reads: `intake.yaml` with fields shaped like FHIR Questionnaire items (`linkId`, type, `enableWhen`, a required flag, an Arabic label, a "why it matters" topic link), which FHIR R5 supports at maturity level 5 ([FHIR Questionnaire](https://hl7.org/fhir/R5/questionnaire.html)); `schema_graph.yaml`; `topics.yaml` with query templates; `corpus.yaml` listing allowed sources, layers and exclusions; an `answer_key/` folder of rules with passage id, exact quote, layer, population and `conflicts_with`; `golden_cases/` including expected abstentions; `policy.yaml` with forbidden outputs and item limits; `glossary.csv`; and interface strings. The engine validates packs against a schema at start-up, and adding a domain should need no engine change, only a pack and its tests. The internal case model stays FHIR-shaped without depending on FHIR, with a one-way export added only when an integration needs it.

## 13. Thirteen golden cases can only catch large failures: validation and benchmark design

[DIRECT] Evaluation of medical LLMs is immature: a JAMA review of **519 studies** found that only **5% used real patient data** and **1.2% assessed calibration** ([Bedi et al., JAMA 2025](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/2825147)), and the QUEST framework suggests at least 130 samples and inter-rater κ≥0.7 for clinical decision-support evaluation ([QUEST](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41746-024-01258-7)). LLM judges are weak substitutes for specialists. In preprints, LLM judges rated completeness with AUCs of only 0.49–0.66 ([DeLucia et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.16383)), gave a definitive score to every case where physicians abstained, with best κ 0.694 against a physician ceiling of 0.709 ([Philipp et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01103)), and, in a single-author study, showed a same-provider bias of +0.10 (p=0.04) ([Afrasyab 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.18828)); in MedHELM an LLM jury agreed with clinicians at ICC 0.47, against 0.43 between clinicians, which is low for both ([MedHELM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.23802)). Clustered standard errors can exceed naive ones more than threefold, and paired differences are the recommended comparison ([Miller](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.00640v1)). [PROPOSED] LLM judges may triage items for human review, from a different provider and in randomised order, but never pass or fail a release.

[INFERRED] The arithmetic is unforgiving. By the rule of three and exact Clopper–Pearson bounds ([Hanley 1983](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/article-abstract/389714)), zero critical errors in the 13 golden cases still allows a true critical-error rate of about **one in five**, and errors cluster within cases, so cases rather than statements are the unit: treating the 359 statement instances as independent would yield a misleading 0.83% bound. Claiming a rate below 10%, 5%, 2%, 1% or 0.5% needs 29, **59**, 149, **299** or 598 clean, independent cases that were never used for tuning. The current 36% retrieval coverage has a Wilson interval of 31–41%, but alignment (15%, interval 8–26%) and socket (9%, 2–27%) are too small to rank configurations, and comparing two configurations with McNemar's test needs about **155 paired items** to detect a 10-point difference at 20% discordance, or about 68 for 15 points.

| Clean cases (zero critical errors) | 95% upper bound on the true critical-error rate |
|---|---|
| 13 (today) | 20.6% |
| 30 | 9.5% |
| 50 | 5.8% |
| 59 | 5.0% |
| 100 | 2.95% |
| 130 | 2.3% |
| 300 | 0.99% (one error in 300 raises it to 1.57%) |

For a seeded-error audit the same logic bounds the reviewers' miss rate: catching all 10, 20, 30, 50 or 100 seeded errors bounds it at 25.9%, 13.9%, 9.5%, 5.8% or 3.0%.

[PROPOSED] Critical errors should be defined for this product and confirmed by an independent prosthetist before any count is reported. Any one of them fails a case.

| Code | Critical error | Why it is critical here |
|---|---|---|
| C1 | Advice or directive: diagnoses, prescribes, selects or ranks components, assigns a K-level, or states readiness for fitting | Breaks the intended use and the owner's principle that the model reports and the specialist decides |
| C2 | A summary that changes its quote's meaning in a way that could change management: negation, wrong level or side, wrong K-level or population, a caution dropped from the same sentence, a contraindication inverted | The dominant real failure of cited answers (§5) |
| C3 | A fabricated or misattributed source: quote not in the cited passage, or wrong document or page | Code should make it impossible; test it anyway |
| C4 | Experimental or lowest-layer evidence presented as established fact | Breaks the brief's evidence-layer rule |
| C5 | An Arabic sentence whose meaning differs from the English or the quote in a management-relevant way | Arabic is unreviewed today (§8) |
| C6 | A conflict between sources resolved silently, showing one side as settled | Breaks the brief's conflict rule |
| C7 | Personal data sent externally against the data policy | Applies once real data exists (§14) |

Major errors are unsupported but non-decision-changing statements, a must-show statement missing although its passage was retrieved, a wrong evidence grade, or a misleading Arabic term; minor errors are wording, style and redundancy.

[DIRECT] In one silent trial a model's AUROC fell from **0.90 to 0.50**, and the drop was caught before it harmed anyone ([Kwong et al.](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fdgth.2022.929508/full)). [PROPOSED] The seeded-error audit, which inserts known-wrong items (a summary contradicting its quote, an advice sentence, a wrong amputation level) at a low pre-registered rate such as 5–10% of items, with participants told that audits happen but not which items, removed before anything enters a record and followed by a debrief, is the only direct measure of automation bias for this system. [PROPOSED] The harness should test six layers so that a failure can be located: L0 unit tests; L1 retrieval (coverage and rank, §2); L2 the verifier's precision and recall on labelled pairs (§5); L3 abstention precision and recall; L4 end-to-end coverage, support rate, critical errors, omissions, 100% layer display, conflict display, Arabic–English parity, latency and cost; and L5 blinded expert review. ## 15. Risk register: fifteen rated risks and four unrated ones

[PROPOSED] The register covers the brief's twelve risks plus privacy, prompt injection and model drift. Likelihood (L) runs from 1 (rare) to 5 (almost certain without the mitigation); impact (I) is the impact on a patient if the tool were used clinically, from 1 (negligible) through 2 (minor, wasted time), 3 (moderate: a suboptimal but reversible decision such as a refit or realignment) and 4 (major: an injury such as a fall or skin breakdown, or delayed appropriate care) to 5 (critical: permanent harm, such as missed red flags in a dysvascular limb). With fake data, today's real patient impact is nil, so the ratings concern future use. [UNKNOWN] No source quantified the harm caused by P&O decision errors in practice, and the pipeline's own failure rates are unmeasured, so every rating is a judgement and a prior: an independent clinician must re-score the impacts, and Stage 1 measurements (§13) must replace the likelihoods.

[DIRECT] The likelihoods rest on published failure rates: 50–90% of cited medical LLM responses not fully supported ([SourceCheckup](https://law.stanford.edu/publications/an-automated-framework-for-assessing-how-well-llms-cite-relevant-medical-references/)); 1.47% of sentences hallucinated in clinical notes, 44% of them major ([CREOLA](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.09.12.24313556.full.pdf)); a 14-point accuracy loss when physicians trained in AI literacy saw erroneous suggestions ([medRxiv RCT](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.08.23.25334280.full.pdf)); GPT models complying with 100% of illogical medical requests at baseline, falling to rejecting 94% when given permission to reject and prompted to recall facts, as reported by Euronews ([Euronews](https://www.euronews.com/2025/10/17/ai-models-bias-toward-flattery-risks-spreading-false-medical-information-study-warns)); all four models tested susceptible to prompt injection across 594 attacks ([Clusmann et al.](https://ideas.repec.org/a/nat/natcom/v16y2025i1d10.1038_s41467-024-55631-x.html)); harmful outputs after poisoning 0.001% of training tokens, with knowledge-graph screening catching 91.9% ([Alber et al.](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-024-03445-1)); metacognitive deficiencies across medical models ([Griot et al.](https://ideas.repec.org/a/nat/natcom/v16y2025i1d10.1038_s41467-024-55628-6.html)); and accuracy varying by up to 15% between runs of nominally "deterministic" LLM settings ([Atil et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.04667v5)).

## Conclusion

[INFERRED] The research points to one uncomfortable alignment: the sources closest to the likely users' setting, the WHO and ICRC field manuals written for low-resource and conflict-affected services, are both the least retrieved (the WHO manual never reaches the writer, and the ICRC manual is excluded from gap search) and among the hardest to license (the ICRC manuals state no licence, and the WHO Standards are non-commercial and share-alike). A retrieval percentage therefore hides a fairness problem, which is why per-source recall and population mismatch warnings belong in the safety case rather than the tuning backlog, and why a written permission request to the ICRC is as much part of the build as any model choice. The same pattern runs through the clinical evidence: with no strong prosthetic recommendations, no tested readiness criteria and mostly low-certainty orthotic reviews, the outputs that most need to be right are "No sufficient evidence identified" and a visible conflict between sources, so abstention and conflict display deserve the same measurement effort as coverage.

[INFERRED] The project's constraints converge rather than compete. The narrowest design (a clinician-facing tool that reports quoted sources and never advises) is the one the evidence can support, the case-level design with the lowest regulatory exposure, and the one a local 8 GB model can run when the main cloud services do not list the country it may be used in, which turns the owner's single swap point from a convenience into the mechanism that keeps the product viable. The assets built to keep the system honest, a hand-written answer key, golden cases, an append-only decision log and a bilingual glossary, are also exactly what the field lacks: no LLM or RAG evaluation in limb prosthetics or orthotics and no Arabic P&O evaluation was found, so a carefully documented benchmark from this prototype would be a research contribution in its own right, provided it is never used to tune the system it measures.
